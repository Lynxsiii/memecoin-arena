# The Memecoin Casino Pays Insiders First

Solana memecoins are a negative-sum game whose reliable profits go to fee collectors and to wallets that are in the token's creation block, not to anyone choosing coins minutes later. pump.fun has booked about **$1.22 billion** in cumulative revenue, trading terminals have taken hundreds of millions more, sandwich bots extracted an estimated **$370–500 million** in 16 months, and deployer-funded snipers win **87%** of their trades by selling within a minute; meanwhile **93.75% of 304,161 active Solana memecoin wallets lost money** over a 90-day window in 2026, and across every multi-month snapshot since 2024 only about **1 in 20 wallets ever realized more than $1,000**. The hypothesis that "every coin goes to zero but most ride for a while first" is half right: nearly every coin does die, but most never ride, since **69% of launches never trade again after launch day** and **60% of the roughly 1% that graduate lose 80% of their value within 20 minutes** of migrating. No published strategy shows a positive, cost-inclusive edge on out-of-sample data; the best measured results are a filtered copy-trading system that keeps **~3% per trade** of a 14% leader edge, and a 190-trade momentum system whose entire profit came from 3 trades. For a $1,000 account each round trip costs roughly **5–8%** on a Fomo-style fee schedule, so a coin must move high single digits just to break even, and the base rates put doubling in the low single-digit percent of wallets and a 10x below half a percent. The arena's result (Claudes avoid rugs but barely profit; rule bots and wallet-copiers lose double digits) is therefore the expected outcome of a well-behaved filter in a market with no picker's edge, not a tuning failure. A Claude that doubles its money is plausible only as a rug-vetoing filter and regime-router wrapped around a mechanical post-migration strategy with a realistic cost model, event-based exits, and several hundred trades of evidence; any "almost guaranteed profit" claim in this market is fraud, an insider position, or survivorship marketing.

## A pump.fun coin is a 14x ramp that 99% never finish

A pump.fun token is free to create and trades on a constant-product bonding curve with a **1.25% fee per trade (0.95% protocol, 0.30% creator)** until roughly **85 SOL of net buying (about $69,000 market cap)** fills the curve, at which point it migrates for 0.015 SOL to pump.fun's own AMM, PumpSwap, which replaced Raydium as the graduation venue in March 2025 ([pump.fun docs](https://pump.fun/docs/fees); [Marino et al., arXiv 2602.14860](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14860)). The curve's price path is deterministic and convex in cumulative SOL deposited, so "late entry is mechanically penalized," and a coin that completes the curve has risen on the order of **10x or more from its birth price by construction** ([arXiv 2602.14860](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14860)); a worked example from 2024 shows $100 bought at a $5,000 market cap returning $1,380 if sold exactly at graduation ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/memecoin-casino-bet-pump-odds)). That mechanical ramp is the only "guaranteed ride" in the system, and it exists only for graduates. After migration PumpSwap fees are tiered by market cap, from **1.25% for coins under 420 SOL of market cap down to 0.30% above 98,240 SOL**, with the creator's slice ranging from 0.95% to 0.05% ([pump.fun docs](https://pump.fun/docs/fees); [Smithii](https://smithii.io/en/project-ascend-update/)). Because the first PumpSwap tier sits just below the graduation cap, any post-migration dump drops a coin straight into the highest-fee bracket: fees are maximal exactly where most retail volume happens and cheapest only for the rare coins that reach ~$20 million.

The supply side is industrial. Launches ran **17,000 to 34,000 per day** through 2025–2026 (record 71,735 on January 23, 2025; **18.67 million lifetime by June 2026**), and graduation rates oscillated between **0.5% and 2%** for two years (2.1% peak in June 2024, 0.63% in September 2025, 0.84% in April 2026, 1.02% over the full January 2024–January 2026 baseline) ([The Block via TradingView](https://ru.tradingview.com/news/the_block%3Af76fef498094b%3A0-pump-fun-sees-rapid-decline-in-graduating-tokens-as-memecoin-frenzy-fizzles-out); [Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/pump-fun-token-survival-rate-study/); [PumpFunData](https://pumpfundata.com/blog/pumpfun-graduation-rate-analysis); [Szwajcok et al., arXiv 2609.10246](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10246)). pump.fun has kept changing the rules to coax more activity: Project Ascend dynamic creator fees (September 2025) paid creators **$2 million in their first day versus $198,000 the day before** ([CoinMarketCap Academy](https://coinmarketcap.com/academy/article/pumpfun-creators-earn-dollar2m-in-first-day-under-new-fee-structure)); Mayhem Mode (November 2025) let an AI agent trade new coins for 24 hours and moved launches by an "insignificant" 2.9% ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/379285/pump-funs-new-mayhem-mode-fails-boost-token-launches-revenue)); and BOOST (late July 2026) now deploys about 20% of migration capital as **automatic market buys during the first five minutes after migration, burning every token bought**, which lifted the graduation rate from ~0.84% to **4.7–6.7%** ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/amp/post/409815/pump-fun-token-graduation-rate-jumps-boost-changes-launch-incentives)). By September 2026 the ecosystem was logging **1,081–1,181 graduations per day on ~33,000 launches**, roughly 3.5% ([Memecoinist](https://memecoinist.com/pump-fun-token-graduation-rate-solana-traders/)). Two caveats matter for a simulator: about **24% of April 2026 graduations were bot graduations** that bonded in under 60 seconds with a median of six buyers, so a quarter of "graduations" have no real counterparties ([PumpFunData](https://pumpfundata.com/blog/pumpfun-graduation-rate-analysis)), and any arena calibrated on pre-July-2026 data will understate the first five minutes of post-migration upside that BOOST now manufactures.

Fomo, the app whose fees the arena models, is a social trading app rather than an automated copier: following a trader shows their positions and sends alerts, but "you still manually decide whether to respond," and Fomo pays Solana network fees on the user's behalf ([Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/fomo-app-explained)). Its Solana fee schedule is tiered by order size according to a secondary source (the primary help page could not be retrieved): **flat 0.10 USDC under 5 USDC, 2% from 5 to 47.50 USDC, flat 0.95 USDC from 47.50 to 190 USDC, and 0.50% at 190 USDC or more** (0.45% with a referral code) ([Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/fomo-referral-code)). The company raised a $75 million Series B in June 2026 and hit $1.57 million in weekly revenue ([Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/fomo-social-trading-app-hits-157m-weekly-revenue-all-time-high-on-solana)). The terminals the pros use charge about **1% per side** (Photon 1%, GMGN 1%, Axiom 1% tiered down to 0.75%, Trojan 1% with cashback), BullX suspended trading on June 1, 2026, and Axiom was accused in February 2026 of employees using internal "God mode" dashboards to front-run users ([MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/best-solana-sniping-tools); [MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/best-bullx-alternatives); [Axiompedia](https://axiompedia.com/guides/fees/solana-terminal-fee-index)). The point of these numbers is not the exact percentages but the structure: the trader pays the launchpad, the AMM, the app, the validator, and the MEV searcher on every single round trip, before any question of skill arises.

## The house and the first block collect the profit

Follow the money and the winners are structural, not predictive. pump.fun's cumulative revenue reached **$1.222 billion by August 2026**, with a peak of **$15.4 million in a single day** on January 25, 2025 ([CryptoTimes](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/08/04/pump-fun-hits-highest-weekly-revenue-since-march/); [The Block via TradingView](https://ru.tradingview.com/news/the_block%3Af76fef498094b%3A0-pump-fun-sees-rapid-decline-in-graduating-tokens-as-memecoin-frenzy-fizzles-out)). Axiom, the dominant terminal, took **$150 million in fees by July 2025 with 72% of Solana bot volume** and crossed $200 million within 202 days of launch, while Photon had collected over $400 million and BullX about $200 million ([DL News](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/defi/solana-bot-axiom-takes-over-cursor-with-150m-revenue/); [Step Data](https://stepdata.substack.com/p/solana-trading-bot-axiom-becomes)). Sandwich bots extracted **$370–500 million from Solana DEX users between January 2024 and May 2025**; a single program ran **1.55 million sandwiches in 30 days for 65,880 SOL (~$13.4 million), about $8.67 per attack**, and 16 of the 20 most-sandwiched tokens were pump.fun coins, with victims being "memecoin traders using high slippage tolerances" ([Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/learn/accelerate-25/scale-or-die-at-accelerate-2025-the-state-of-solana-mev); [Helius](https://www.helius.dev/blog/solana-mev-report)). Even the "user-side" fees are large: Photon users alone were paying 200–400 SOL per day in Jito tips in April 2025 ([Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/jitos-role-in-solana-deep-dive)). Added together, platform, terminal, and MEV extraction from memecoin order flow is on the order of **$3 billion** since 2024, before priority fees and slippage. That is the house edge every trader, human or AI, is competing against.

The second group of reliable winners is anyone who holds supply before the public can buy. Pine Analytics traced **over 15,000 pump.fun launches in one month where a same-block sniper had received SOL directly from the deployer**, involving 4,600 sniper wallets and 10,400 deployers; those snipes realized **15,000+ SOL of net profit with an 87% win rate**, and **55% of them were fully exited within one minute, 85% within five** ([Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines)). That subset only catches direct funding links, so it is a floor. Across 41,470 migrated tokens, **bundled accounts held 36.5% of total supply** and 98.7% of developers bought in the same transaction as minting ([MemeTrans, arXiv 2602.13480](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480v1)). Creators run factories: the top 1% of creator clusters account for **53–59% of all 15 million coins**, 23.5% of coins were created right after a social post, wash trading accounts for **50% of volume in coins with more than 10,000 transactions**, and there is a documented "market-manipulation-as-a-service" tooling market ([arXiv 2609.10246](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.10246)). One creator reportedly made **$840,000 launching 17,000 tokens in three months** ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/303439/crypto-trader-840k-17000-meme-coins-three-months?amp=1)). KOLs sit between the two groups: across 1,058 tracked influencer wallets and 1.6 million trades, the median win rate was 57% but wallets made **1.5 buys per sell, bought an average 1.52 SOL and sold an average 2.24 SOL**, and "most bought tokens go to zero and are never sold," a pattern of spraying small bets and selling only the winners that makes published win rates flatter than the true expectancy ([CoinStats/MadeOnSol](https://coinstats.app/news/07f38ebd47e8d820af3eb96b00c42a84b9b856c602626e3e4a71afbbd79014d6_KOL-Wallet-Tracking-on-Solana-What-the-Data-Actually-Shows-After-16-Million-Trades/)).

Everyone else loses, and the magnitude distribution is remarkably stable even as the headline "share profitable" swings with the market. In August 2024, of 2.43 million pump.fun wallets, **3.06% had ever realized more than $1,000, 0.477% more than $10,000, and 70 wallets more than $1 million** ([Bitget/Dune](https://www.bitget.com/news/detail/12560604161427); [ChainCatcher](https://www.chaincatcher.com/en/article/2139008)). Over December 2024–June 2025, 62.5% of 4.2 million wallets had net realized losses, 4.5% made $1k–$10k, and 311 made over $1 million ([crypto.news](https://crypto.news/over-60-pump-fun-traders-saw-losses-less-than-0-01-made-over-1m/)). CoinGecko's monthly series shows the profitable share bottoming at **30.1% in June 2025 and rising to 73.3% in April 2026**, but even in that best month **65% of profitable wallets made $1–$500 and only 5.4% cleared $1,000**, and the improvement coincided with active wallets falling from 5.2 million to 1.8 million, meaning fewer, more professional participants rather than an easier market ([CoinGecko Research](https://www.coingecko.com/research/publications/pump-fun-traders-are-making-a-comeback)). A press-reported 90-day sample to August 2026 (methodology unpublished) found **6.25% of 304,161 wallets profitable, a median loss of $120, 88% of winners under $100, and just 25 wallets above $10,000** ([Cointribune](https://www.cointribune.com/en/crypto-solana-memecoins-trap-nearly-94-of-traders)). All of these dashboards count realized PnL only (so bag-holders who never sold show as break-even), count wallets rather than people, and do not separate bots, which is why "share profitable" should be read loosely; "about one wallet in twenty ever books more than $1,000" is the number that survives every methodology. The game is zero-sum between traders before costs and clearly negative-sum after the extraction documented above, with the aggregate $1.26 billion lost in that 90-day window dwarfing pump.fun's own ~$31 million monthly revenue, which tells you most retail losses are transfers to other traders (deployers, snipers, bundlers) rather than to fee collectors alone.

## Every coin dies, but most never ride first

The data support the first half of the user's hypothesis without qualification. **68.67% of all 18.67 million pump.fun tokens recorded their final trade on the day they were created, and only 4.55% remained active beyond 90 days** ([Crypto Briefing](https://cryptobriefing.com/pump-fun-token-survival-rate-study/)). Of 7 million tokens with at least five trades, 98.6% failed to retain $1,000 of liquidity ([Solidus Labs](https://www.soliduslabs.com/reports/solana-rug-pulls-pump-dumps-crypto-compliance)). Even among the ~150 Solana-era memecoins large enough for a centralized exchange listing, **81% fell at least 90% from their all-time high and only 3.3% ever regained it** ([Coin Metrics](https://coinmetrics.substack.com/p/state-of-the-network-issue-384)). The tail is tiny: as of August 2024, 96 tokens out of 1.8 million had ever exceeded $1 million in market cap and 18 had exceeded $10 million, roughly one in 100,000 ([Bitget/Dune](https://www.bitget.com/news/detail/12560604161427)).

The second half, "most ride for a while first," fails. Most launches never move at all, and among those that do, the ride is measured in minutes and is already crowded. The **median hold time of a Solana memecoin position is about 100 seconds**, down from 300 a year earlier ([Galaxy via Cointelegraph/TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/news/cointelegraph:fbf71a3df094b:0-memecoins-attract-users-but-platforms-reap-the-profits-report)). **92.22% of tokens with at least 30 trades show at least one statistically significant dump** on the curve, and selling strictly before graduation is more profitable than selling just after because the curve's virtual liquidity is deeper than the fresh AMM pool, so insiders are incentivized to dump into the migration ([arXiv 2602.14860](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14860)). Graduation itself takes a **median of 4.4 to 9.8 minutes**, with 62% of organic graduates bonding within 10 minutes and 85% within an hour, and it takes a median **281 SOL of gross buying to produce the 85 SOL of net inflow**, so the curve is churned roughly three times over by traders flipping on the way up ([arXiv 2602.14860](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14860); [PumpFunData](https://pumpfundata.com/blog/pumpfun-graduation-rate-analysis)). After migration the collapse is immediate: **60.26% of 41,470 migrated tokens fell below 20% of their migration price within 20 minutes, 73% fell below 40%, and only 4.53% held 70% or better**, with the early drops attributed to "insiders unwinding their low-cost inventory" ([MemeTrans](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480v1)). Sixty days out, **91% of 2,891 migrations sat at 20% or less of migration price and only 6.2% were above it** ([Memecoinist](https://memecoinist.com/pump-fun-token-graduation-rate-solana-traders/)). A vendor dataset puts the **median 2026 graduate's peak market cap at $31,000, below the ~$69,000 graduation cap**, meaning a buyer at or after graduation is more likely than not already above the token's eventual high ([MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/pump-fun-first-3-minutes-graduation-math)).

Where a ride exists, its shape is now reasonably well measured, and it rewards early entry and punishes chasing. In a cohort of 265 pump events (a day of +100% or more on at least $150,000 of volume), the **median time from pump start to peak was 22 hours, 63% peaked inside the pump day, and the median retrace from peak was 60% within 72 hours**; followers who entered within an hour of a smart wallet were **+62% at 24 hours and +79% at 7 days (median)**, while chasers who bought at the day's close were **−18% at 24 hours and −29% at 72 hours**; 65% of early entries were profitable after a 4% round-trip cost, and 37% of pumped tokens had another +100% day within 30 days ([Unhosted Research](https://www.unhosted.ai/research/memecoin-pump-anatomy)). For the few survivors that reach CEX scale, the median all-time high comes 17.2 days after first trade, with a quarter peaking inside 1.6 days ([Coin Metrics](https://coinmetrics.substack.com/p/state-of-the-network-issue-384)). The honest restatement of the hypothesis is therefore: every coin eventually goes to roughly zero; a filtered minority (on the order of 1–5% of launches, and perhaps 5–10% of graduates) rides at all; that ride is front-loaded into minutes or hours and its first minute is owned by same-block snipers; the sign of a trade is decided by entry timing more than by coin choice; and the 37% repeat-pump tail argues for trailing or event-based exits rather than fixed time exits. "Catch the wave" is possible in principle only for an agent that is not chasing and that can get out on the first insider sell.

## Measured signals tell you what to avoid, not what will run

Academic and practitioner work on pump.fun has converged on a set of signals, and nearly all the robust ones are negative filters. The strongest out-of-sample finding is that **trading intensity (reaching a given curve level in few swaps) and a high share of non-bot trades (at least 70% through front-ends rather than direct contract calls) predict graduation**, identified on the first two weeks of September 2025 and tested on the second two ([arXiv 2602.14860](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14860)). Early-holder concentration is a reliable risk marker: the first 10 buyers of high-risk tokens hold 17 percentage points more supply than in low-risk tokens, and feature importance ranks market activity above bundle statistics above holding concentration above launch timing ([MemeTrans](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480v1)). Creator history matters: a reverse-engineered leading sniper bot in 2024 never bought if the creator had launched coins before, was funded by prior creators, or made an initial buy over 4 SOL, and it exited instantly if the creator sold ([mikem.codes](https://www.mikem.codes/if-you-aint-first-youre-last-2/)); a vendor's "dev previously graduated a token and has been quiet for 7 days" filter graduated **12% versus a 1.7% baseline in-sample but 6.5% in a 77-position live probe** ([ScorpLabs](https://trader.scorplabs.online/blog/pump-fun-graduation-rate-dev-signal-12-percent)). Market cap at entry raises graduation odds, but the unconditional graduation probability "lies below the breakeven curve" over most of the curve's range, so buying higher does not pay for the worse price ([arXiv 2602.14860](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14860)).

The cautionary tales are as important as the positive findings. Social links looked enormous in-sample (a Telegram link carried an 8.9x graduation lift and a Cox hazard ratio of 5.40 across 832,941 launches), yet the same author's pre-registered follow-up saw the model's **AUROC collapse from 0.86 in development to 0.46 on the next two weeks**, inside the no-skill interval, and now states these features "are not predictors of pump.fun graduation" ([Kamat, arXiv 2607.02823 v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02823v3); [v4 audit](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.02823)). KOL mentions are, on average, a sell signal: across 377 influencers and 1,567 promoted memecoins, **80% of tokens were down 70% a week after promotion, 86% were at a tenth of the promotion price after three months, and only 1–3% ever hit 10x**, with the largest accounts performing worst ([BlockBeats](https://en.theblockbeats.news/news/55919)). Time of day did not reach significance in the one paper that tested it (p = 0.56) ([arXiv 2606.08232](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08232)). "Unique buyers" and raw volume are the two most manipulated observables, inflated by sniper rings, bump bots, and wash trading, so SOL-denominated net inflow and swaps-per-curve-level are the better demand proxies. Machine-learning results with temporal holdouts are all classifiers for bad outcomes rather than return predictors: an XGBoost model on the first five minutes predicts a rug within the hour at **F1 0.78 / MCC 0.36** on pump.fun but transfers at MCC ≈ 0 across platforms ([arXiv 2608.20271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20271)), and MemeTrans's risk score cuts simulated losses "by up to 56%" on a non-temporal split ([MemeTrans](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480v1)). Feature drift is the dominant failure mode, so any model must be retrained on rolling windows of weeks.

The one published, cost-aware entry system illustrates what an edge looks like here, if it exists at all. A momentum-and-liquidity system paper-traded 190 positions over two weeks in 2026 with a **40.5% win rate, +0.62% mean return per trade, −3.58% median, and a cumulative +117.7% that equated to a net of only +0.039 SOL**; removing the top three trades (1.6% of the sample) flips the cumulative return negative, winners were held a median 3.6 minutes against 0.6 for losers, and slippage, failed transactions and MEV were not modeled ([arXiv 2606.08232](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08232)). This is the statistical signature of the whole market: a strongly negative median with a heavy right tail, where "momentum" pays only by harvesting rare continuation runs and only if losers are cut within about a minute. Copy trading, the arena's third team, is better studied and the news is worse. In the only peer-reviewed Solana study, wallets selected as "smart money" averaged **+14% per trade, every statistics-only copying baseline produced negative returns after realistic friction despite correctly identifying profitable wallets, and only a multi-agent system that vetted the wallet, the coin's bot presence, and the entry timing reached ~+3% per trade** ([Luo et al., arXiv 2601.08641](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v2)). Copiers are 1–5 seconds (several blocks) behind, and on an illiquid token "the lead wallet's purchase already moves price upward" before the copy lands ([MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/best-solana-copy-trading-bots)); GMGN's own documentation warns that priority fees "can turn a profitable target into an unprofitable copy" and that "the system cannot determine in advance whether a tracked wallet intends to exit" ([Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/gmgn-explained)). Even on centralized perps with no bonding curve, **97% of lead traders were personally profitable but only 43.6% delivered positive returns to followers** ([KuCoin relaying YieldFund](https://www.kucoin.com/blog/is-crypto-copytrading-profitable-in-2026)). The arena's copy bots losing 11–49% in a day is the same phenomenon the Pine Analytics sniper cohort shows from the other side: a leaderboard sorted by win rate and 24-hour PnL selects for wallets whose edge is same-block position and sub-minute exits, which is precisely the edge a lagging follower cannot share and is instead the exit liquidity for.

## Scams are mostly dumps, and the cheapest checks catch the worst ones

The dominant way buyers lose is not a clever exploit but coordinated selling by the deployer's own wallet cluster. In H1 2025 a preprint flagged **76,469 of 100,063 newly issued tokens on Orca, Raydium and Meteora as rug-pull candidates: 60,402 pump-and-dumps, 15,606 liquidity withdrawals, and 461 freeze-authority abuses**, with a random audit finding a 0.26% false-positive rate and at least $151 million in directly traceable losses ([DeepStrike citing arXiv 2603.24625](https://deepstrike.io/blog/rug-pull-statistics)). Solidus Labs found **93% of 388,000 Raydium pools showed soft-rug behavior (creator withdraws 90%+ of liquidity after traders deposit), with a median incident of $2,832** and a largest of $1.9 million ([Solidus Labs](https://www.soliduslabs.com/reports/solana-rug-pulls-pump-dumps-crypto-compliance)). The widely quoted "98.6% are scams" conflates abandoned coins with fraud, so the ~76% figure with explicit labels is the better prior for "tokens that will be deliberately dumped on you." Two structural facts shape detection: on the bonding curve, liquidity cannot be pulled because the curve holds the SOL, and after graduation PumpSwap burns LP tokens ([DEXTools](https://www.dextools.io/news/pump-fun-graduation-collapse-solana-fees-2026)), so on pump.fun the risk is overwhelmingly holder-cluster dumping, not LP removal.

Honeypots (coins you can buy but not sell) are rarer but total-loss events, and on Solana they are readable directly from the mint account: an active freeze authority lets the creator freeze buyers' token accounts ("can't sell, can't transfer, can't do anything"), an active mint authority allows unlimited inflation, and Token-2022 extensions add permanent delegate (which let a scammer burn a victim's tokens within seven seconds of their swap in a September 2024 case), transfer fees and transfer hooks ([dev.to](https://dev.to/mrvlyouknowwho/freeze-authority-is-the-solana-honeypot-how-to-check-any-spl-token-in-10-seconds-free-no-wallet-15h4); [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/new-solana-scam-burn-tokens-from-inside-sol-wallets)). GoPlus exposes `freezable`, `mintable`, `transfer_fee`, `transfer_hook`, holder concentration and DEX liquidity through a keyless endpoint, and RugCheck returns a structured risk report per mint without an account ([GoPlus docs](https://docs.gopluslabs.io/); [Apidog on RugCheck](https://apidog.com/blog/rugcheck-api/)). Bundling and sniping are the insider-supply problem: Bubblemaps' working thresholds are **bundled supply under 5% low concern, 5–15% moderate, over 15% serious, over 30% avoid**, cluster-linked supply over 50% "extreme risk," and any single bundle holding 10%+ a major red flag, with cross-launch recurrence of the same wallet set the highest-precision tell because it cannot be faked cheaply ([Bubblemaps](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/how-to-analyze-meme-coin-holders-with-bubblemaps/); [Bubblemaps](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/whats-the-difference-between-bundle-cluster-2/)). Mobula labels snipers (buys within 0–3 blocks of creation, fresh wallets funded just before launch) and bundlers (3+ wallets in one atomic transaction, same funder, tight buy sizes, later consolidation) and reports token-level sniper and bundler holdings percentages ([Mobula docs](https://docs.mobula.io/almanac/detecting-snipers-bundlers)). Bubblemaps' investigation linking one entity to **$2.4 million sniped on MELANIA and $6 million on LIBRA**, through side wallets funded cross-chain, shows the pattern at celebrity scale ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/341238/bubblemaps-links-single-entity-to-libra-and-melania-memecoins)).

Fake activity is the norm rather than the exception: **93 of the top 100 pump.fun/PumpSwap wallets by activity are bots**, often active 18+ hours a day ([Crypto Times](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2025/06/10/93-out-of-top-100-wallets-on-pump-fun-are-bots-crypto-sleuth/)), "bump bots" run matched buy-sell pairs to fake volume, and comment bots generate slogan hype that correlates with better reported token performance because the hype works ([Luo et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v2)). Sandwich attacks cost a median victim about $5 and some over $100, with 0.038% of Jito bundles being sandwiches but memecoin pairs the main target ([Gerzon, Weintraub et al., IMC '25](https://ben-weintraub.com/files/solanamev.pdf)); the official defense is tight slippage, Jito's DontFront account marker, and MEV-protect RPC routing that avoids sandwiching validators ([Solana docs](https://solana.com/developers/guides/advanced/mev-protection); [Helius](https://www.helius.dev/docs/sending-transactions/mev-protect.md)). Bait wallets are the copy-trading attack surface: adversaries manufacture a public PnL by self-dealing across controlled wallets on illiquid tokens, attract copiers, and dump, which Luo et al. model as "a near zero-sum game where adversary profits directly correlate with copier losses" ([Luo et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v2)); insider-network fingerprints are shared funding sources, fresh wallet clusters created together with similar balances, wallets that buy and sell in perfect synchronization across tokens, and proceeds converging to one collection wallet ([DeFade](https://defade.org/blog/solana-memecoin-insider-networks)). The sources agree on one meta-point: composite 0–100 "safety scores" produce false positives and negatives, nobody agrees what "passing" means, and deterministic fields should be pulled raw and thresholded by the agent itself rather than trusted as verdicts. The checklist below consolidates the published thresholds into the order an agent should run them, cheapest first.

## A $1,000 account pays 5–8% per round trip and rarely doubles

For a small account the fee stack is not a rounding error; it is the main reason the median trade loses. On Fomo's tiered schedule a **$25 or $50 order pays 2% per side, a $100 order pays the flat $0.95 (about 1%), and only orders of $190 or more reach the 0.5% tier**, which creates a dead zone exactly where sensible position sizes for a $1,000 account live ([Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/fomo-referral-code)). On top of that sit the 1.25% curve fee per side (or 0.30–1.25% on PumpSwap), price impact, and on non-Fomo apps a priority fee plus Jito tip of roughly 0.002–0.005 SOL per transaction ([pump.fun docs](https://pump.fun/docs/fees); [Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/jitos-role-in-solana-deep-dive)). Price impact scales with order size over pool depth: on a fresh curve a 1 SOL buy moves price about 3.3%, falling to 0.9% once 80 SOL is in the curve ([MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/solana-bonding-curves-explained)). Combining those inputs (arithmetic ours, SOL at $150, impact approximated as buy-side plus sell-side impact) gives the following round-trip costs for a coin about 60 SOL into its curve.

| Position | Fomo app fees (both sides) | Curve fees at 1.25% | Price impact | Total round trip |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| $25 | $1.00 | $0.63 | ~$0.10 | ~$1.70 (≈7%) |
| $50 | $1.90 | $1.25 | ~$0.40 | ~$3.55 (≈7%) |
| $100 | $1.90 | $2.50 | ~$1.50 | ~$5.90 (≈6%) |
| $250 | $2.50 | $6.25 | ~$9.00 | ~$17.75 (≈7%); ~13% on a brand-new curve |

A trade therefore needs to move roughly **5–8% in your favor just to break even, and 10–15% for a $250 order into a new curve**, before any adverse movement while the transaction lands. Against a market whose median trade is −3.6% and whose median hold is 100 seconds, fee drag alone explains most of the loser rate. The cheapest execution is a deep post-migration pool: the same $250 order in a 500 SOL pool costs about 3.6% round trip, and Jupiter's direct route charges only 0.5% on tokens under 24 hours old and 0.1% afterwards, though without any terminal tooling ([Jupiter docs](https://developers.jup.ag/docs/ultra/fees)).

Sizing guidance from practitioners is consistent and conservative: **1–5% of bankroll per coin, a hard cap near 10%, 5–10 concurrent positions, and a daily loss limit of 3–5%**, because a 30% drawdown needs +43% to recover, a 50% drawdown needs +100%, and about 70% of retail traders quit after a drawdown of that size ([Altrady](https://www.altrady.com/blog/crypto-trading-strategies/how-to-trade-memecoins); [Blofin Academy](https://blofin.com/academy/education/risk-of-ruin)). Kelly arithmetic explains why those fractions are the ceiling, not the floor: with the binary Kelly formula f* = (p·b − q)/b, a 10% chance of a 10x gives an optimal bet of exactly zero, a 15% chance of a 10x gives 5.6% (2.8% at half-Kelly, which practitioners recommend because edge estimates are unreliable), and a 20% chance of a 5x again gives zero ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/position-sizing-kelly-criterion-2017-05-25)). At the published base rates, in other words, the correct bet on a random launch is nothing; 1–5% sizing is only rational if the agent genuinely has a 25–35% hit rate on 3–5x winners, and the 5–8% round-trip cost shrinks every one of those edges further. For $1,000, $25 bets allow about 40 consecutive total losses before ruin while $100 bets allow 10, which is why the small-account compromise is $25–$50 for lottery-style plays and $100 only for liquid, higher-conviction post-migration tokens, accepting Fomo's 2% per side on the small tickets or concentrating into fewer $190+ orders where impact is low.

Profit-taking rules are practitioner consensus rather than tested results, but they are consistent and they fit the measured price paths. The dominant rule is **sell 50% at 2x to recover the initial stake**, then scale out 20–30% at 3x, 5x and 10x and keep a "moon bag" on a trailing stop; **25–35% trails are described as the sweet spot**, with 15–20% reserved for scalps because memecoins routinely swing 50% on noise; progressive tightening (40% trail until 2x, 30% from 2–5x, 20% from 5–10x, 15% above) and time decay (tighten after 4 hours without a new high, sell the remainder after 24) complete the framework ([DEXTools](https://www.dextools.io/tutorials/memecoin-trading-strategy-take-profits); [MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/solana-memecoin-exit-strategy-guide)). Two measured facts modify this for an automated agent. First, tight percentage stops are gapped through on thin pools, because a single insider sell moves price more than the stop width, so the exit triggers with evidence are event-based: the creator or bundle wallets selling (the one reverse-engineered profitable bot "exit[ed] immediately if creators sold"), liquidity withdrawal, and the migration event itself ([mikem.codes](https://www.mikem.codes/if-you-aint-first-youre-last-2/); [arXiv 2602.14860](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14860)). Second, the base rates justify a time stop: among tokens one system rejected, 56% halved within six hours, and winners were held six times longer than losers, so "cut fast, let the rare continuation run" beats fixed targets ([arXiv 2606.08232](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08232)).

What are the odds? No dataset conditions outcomes on a $1,000 starting balance, so the best available proxies are upper bounds. "Doubling" a $1,000 account corresponds to the **3–6% of wallets that ever realized more than $1,000** (3.1% in August 2024, ~5.6% over December 2024–June 2025, 5.4% in April 2026), and a "10x" corresponds to the **0.4–0.5% of wallets that ever realized more than $10,000**; since many of those wallets deployed far more than $1,000, the conditional probability for a small starter is lower than both figures ([Bitget/Dune](https://www.bitget.com/news/detail/12560604161427); [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/over-60-pump-fun-traders-saw-losses-less-than-0-01-made-over-1m/); [CoinGecko Research](https://www.coingecko.com/research/publications/pump-fun-traders-are-making-a-comeback)). The loss tail is roughly symmetric in count (about 6% of wallets lost more than $1,000 in the 2025 window) and understated because bag-holders are excluded. A realistic planning assumption for a $1,000 account trading actively is negative expectancy per trade before any selection edge, monthly drawdowns of 20–50% as a normal outcome, and a long right tail driven by a handful of trades, which means performance can only be judged over hundreds of trades. Two practical notes for a US-based trader, offered as general information rather than advice: crypto is taxed as property and as of mid-2026 the stock wash-sale rule does not apply to it, though a bill (S. 2207) would change that if enacted ([Harness](https://www.harness.co/articles/crypto-tax-loss-harvesting-in-2026-does-the-wash-sale-rule-still-not-apply)); and because the DeFi broker reporting rule was repealed in April 2025, apps like Fomo will not issue a 1099-DA, so the trader (or the agent's logging) must keep every swap's date, amounts, USD value and fees from day one ([RSM US](https://rsmus.com/insights/tax-alerts/2025/congress-nullifies-irs-crypto-reporting-regulations-for-defi-platforms)).

## LLM traders lose to fees unless they filter instead of pick

Every public live test of general-purpose language models trading crypto shows the same shape as the arena. In Nof1's Alpha Arena (real money, Hyperliquid perps, October–November 2025) six models started with $10,000 each; the published snapshot had **Qwen3 Max +22%, DeepSeek +5%, Claude Sonnet 4.5 −42%, Gemini −46%, Grok −58% and GPT-5 −59%**, with a different outlet reporting smaller losses for the losers (Claude −$3,081), win rates of only 25–30% across all models, Gemini making 238 trades to Claude's 38, and the winner paying the highest fee bill at $1,654; Nof1's own finding was that "PnL was dominated by trading costs in early runs as agents over-traded and took quick, tiny gains that fees erased" ([iWeaver](https://www.iweaver.ai/blog/alpha-arena-ai-trading-season-1-results/); [Protos](https://protos.com/llm-crypto-trading-contest-finds-llms-cant-trade-crypto/)). In the AI-Trader live benchmark all six LLM agents lost on crypto, with only DeepSeek beating a −14% index, and the authors concluded that "general LLM intelligence does not translate to trading proficiency" and that "risk management, not return maximization, determines cross-market success" ([AI-Trader, arXiv 2512.10971](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10971v1)). A three-stage evaluation of 32 methods found that **rankings reversed between backtest and paper trading, only one of four deployed methods stayed profitable in live trading (+3.72%), none beat Bitcoin buy-and-hold (+11.63%), LLM decision latency was 187–523 seconds per decision, and live execution prices diverged from paper by six times the expected amount** ([Yu et al., arXiv 2609.34510](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34510v1)). On analysis tasks, agents score 55–59% on simple retrieval but **6–29% on complex prediction**, "sophisticated search engines rather than true analysts" ([CryptoBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.00417v5)). The "AI agent" tokens that rode the 2025 narrative never published a trading record: ai16z's founder declared "the token is dead" in August 2026 after a class action alleging insider control ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/08/05/ai-agent-token-once-worth-usd2-4-billion-ends-with-founder-calling-it-dead)), and AIXBT lost 55.5 ETH when an attacker fed malicious replies into its autonomous action loop ([Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/310510/aixbt-ai-influencer-hacked-100k-ethereum)).

The most useful study for the arena is the only published real-capital LLM memecoin deployment. DX Terminal Pro ran Qwen3-235B agents over 3,505 user-funded vaults on 12 Base memecoins for 21 days, and its pre-launch testing catalogued exactly the failure modes a Claude arena should expect: **57% of sell decisions cited invented constraints** such as a nonexistent "Hierarchy rule #2," **32.5% of observations were blocked by "fee paralysis"** because fee language appeared before market context, soft guidance inverted a trading slider, and agents treated each polling interval as a signal ("cadence trading"). Harness-only fixes (fees stated first and relative to expected daily volatility, no law-like phrasing, prior decisions framed as context not precedent, comparative language instead of exact thresholds, memory filtering so repeated observations do not become a rhythm) cut fabricated rules from **57% to 3%** and raised capital deployment from **42.9% to 78%** ([arXiv 2604.26091](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.26091)). A May 2026 survey of agentic trading adds look-ahead leakage through memory, strategy crowding when several agents share objectives, and the need to decouple slow reasoning from fast execution, and notes that only 1 of 19 empirical studies even specified transaction costs ([arXiv 2605.19337](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19337v1)). Read against these results, the arena's Claudes avoiding rugs but topping out at +2.2% in a day is the expected profile of a cautious, low-frequency model (Claude made the fewest trades in Alpha Arena and lost the least in one tally) operating in a market where most organic tokens die and the fee stack eats small wins; it is a harness and market-structure outcome, not evidence that the model cannot see good coins.

The realistic job description follows directly. An LLM cannot be a launch sniper, because organic graduations complete in a median 9.8 minutes with insiders in the creation block, while its own decision takes minutes ([PumpFunData](https://pumpfundata.com/blog/pumpfun-graduation-rate-analysis); [Yu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34510v1)), and it has no demonstrated price-prediction skill. What it has is judgment over structured evidence, which is where the "avoid rugs" result comes from. The three roles with support in the literature are a rug and manipulation classifier over typed, deterministically computed features (the Tier 0–2 checks above); a narrative reader over token metadata and social text with an explicit "do nothing" default; and a regime router that chooses which mechanical strategy a fast executor should run, rather than placing orders itself. The place such a pipeline could plausibly make money is post-migration persistence: tokens that still hold liquidity and organic buyers one to several hours after graduation, bought after the snipers have exited (55% within a minute, 85% within five), held with event-based exits, sized small enough that the fee dead zone and a single bad fill cannot erase ten good trades. On "almost guaranteed profit" claims the verdict is unambiguous. The SEC charged schemes that lured victims with "AI-generated investment tips" and fictitious profit screens ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025-144-sec-charges-three-purported-crypto-asset-trading-platforms-four-investment-clubs-scheme-targeted)) and, in September 2026, operators who misappropriated over $12.5 million and $2.8 million through fake AI signals and nonexistent bots ([Cryptonomist](https://en.cryptonomist.ch/2026/09/30/sec-investment-fraud-charges/)); the only documented near-certain edge on pump.fun is the deployer-funded sniper ring at 87%, which is an insider position rather than a model; and the best public LLM traders posted 25–30% win rates. Anything marketed as guaranteed is fraud, insider access, or a survivorship-filtered screenshot, and the sources' evaluation rule is that a black-box bot with promised returns is malicious until a time-stamped, wallet-addressable, fee-inclusive trade log proves otherwise.

## What to change in the arena, in priority order

The recommendations below are ordered by how much each one changes the believability of the arena's numbers, not by effort. The first two are prerequisites: until the simulator charges realistic costs and the agents stop being asked to predict, every result (good or bad) is uninterpretable.

| Priority | Change | Why (evidence) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Make the fill and cost model honest: Fomo's size tiers (2% per side under $47.50, flat $0.95 to $190, 0.5% above), 1.25% curve fee or the PumpSwap market-cap tier, price impact from actual pool depth (≈ trade ÷ reserve, not a flat %), a failed-transaction rate, a sandwich penalty when slippage is set high, and a "sniper exit" adverse-move penalty for any entry inside the first 60 seconds. Add a five-minute BOOST buy pulse after migration. | Paper-to-live price discrepancy was 6x in the one controlled study ([Yu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34510v1)); fee drag dominated Alpha Arena PnL ([Protos](https://protos.com/llm-crypto-trading-contest-finds-llms-cant-trade-crypto/)); the Fomo dead zone means $25–$50 bets pay ~7% round trip ([Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/fomo-referral-code)); BOOST changed the post-migration path in July 2026 ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/amp/post/409815/pump-fun-token-graduation-rate-jumps-boost-changes-launch-incentives)). |
| 2 | Change the Claudes' job from "pick runners" to "veto, then route": Claude runs the Tier 0–4 checklist on typed features and outputs reject/size/strategy; a deterministic executor handles entries and exits at sub-second speed. Give Claude an explicit "do nothing" default and state fees first, relative to expected volatility, with no hard numeric thresholds in the prompt. | Every temporal-holdout ML result is a bad-outcome classifier, not a return predictor ([arXiv 2608.20271](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20271)); harness fixes cut fabricated rules 57%→3% ([arXiv 2604.26091](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.26091)); LLM latency is minutes while the edge is same-block ([PumpFunData](https://pumpfundata.com/blog/pumpfun-graduation-rate-analysis)). |
| 3 | Replace price signals with structural ones in the agents' context: net SOL inflow vs gross volume, swaps per curve level, non-bot trade share, unique funded buyers after cluster dedupe, bundled and cluster supply %, top-10 share, deployer history (prior tokens, graduation ratio, still holding, initial buy size), creator-sold and bundle-sold events, LP burn status, DexScreener boost/ad/order status, minutes since migration, and liquidity persistence at 1h/6h/24h. Drop raw trade counts, social-link presence, KOL mentions and time of day as positive inputs. | Trading intensity and non-bot share are the only signals robust out of sample ([arXiv 2602.14860](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.14860)); social links collapsed from AUROC 0.86 to 0.46 on holdout ([arXiv 2607.02823](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.02823)); KOL promotions are 80% down 70% within a week ([BlockBeats](https://en.theblockbeats.news/news/55919)); DexScreener exposes paid-promotion status per token ([DexScreener API](https://docs.dexscreener.com/api/reference)). |
| 4 | Trade only the post-migration persistence window: candidates are graduates that passed Tier 0–2, are at least 5–15 minutes past migration, show bundle wallets already sold, and still hold liquidity and organic net inflow; never buy on the curve or inside the first minute after a launch or migration. | 60% of graduates lose 80% within 20 minutes as insiders unwind ([MemeTrans](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.13480v1)); 55% of snipers exit within a minute ([Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines)); early followers +62% vs chasers −18% ([Unhosted](https://www.unhosted.ai/research/memecoin-pump-anatomy)). |
| 5 | Mechanical sizing and exits: $25–$50 per lottery-style position (2.5–5%), $100 hard cap for liquid post-migration tokens, 5–10 concurrent, 3–5% daily loss cap that halts the agent; sell 50% at 2x, scale 25% at 3x and 5x, 25–35% trailing stop on the remainder that tightens with each multiple; time stop if no new high within the strategy's window; hard exits on creator sell, bundle-wallet sell, or liquidity withdrawal; minimum landing slippage, never a blanket 30–50%. | Kelly at base-rate odds is zero and half-Kelly at optimistic odds is ~3% ([Nasdaq](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/position-sizing-kelly-criterion-2017-05-25)); practitioner consensus on 2x/trail/time-decay ([MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/solana-memecoin-exit-strategy-guide)); the profitable sniper bot exited on creator sell ([mikem.codes](https://www.mikem.codes/if-you-aint-first-youre-last-2/)); winners held 6x longer than losers ([arXiv 2606.08232](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08232)). |
| 6 | Rebuild the Copy team as a filtered shadow of a few vetted leaders (rules in the text below), not a mirror of the top-100 leaderboard. | Statistics-only copying is negative after friction; filtered copying reaches ~3%/trade ([Luo et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v2)); leaderboards surface 87%-win-rate snipers ([Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines)). |
| 7 | Pre-register and gate: replay on pumpfundata Parquet, then ≥4 weeks live paper, then tiny live, with the benchmarks and statistics listed below, and vary prompts and roles across the seven Claudes so the arena is not measuring one strategy seven times. | Rankings reversed between backtest, paper and live ([Yu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.34510v1)); 1 of 19 studies specified costs, agents herd ([arXiv 2605.19337](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19337v1)); hourly event dumps exist for offline replay ([PumpFunData](https://pumpfundata.com/blog/pumpfun-graduation-rate-analysis)). |

For data, the free tiers are sufficient for a minutes-cadence arena and paying for sub-second feeds buys nothing the model can use: PumpPortal's `subscribeNewToken` and `subscribeMigration` streams are free (trade streams are metered and only one connection is allowed) ([PumpPortal](https://pumpportal.fun/data-api/real-time/)), Helius's free tier gives 10 requests per second with webhooks ([Helius](https://www.helius.dev/pricing)), DexScreener allows 60 requests per minute and exposes liquidity, buy/sell counts and boost/ad status ([DexScreener API](https://docs.dexscreener.com/api/reference)), GoPlus and RugCheck are keyless ([GoPlus](https://docs.gopluslabs.io/); [Apidog](https://apidog.com/blog/rugcheck-api/)), and pumpfundata publishes hourly Parquet dumps of all bonding-curve and AMM events since February 2026 for offline replay ([PumpFunData](https://pumpfundata.com/blog/pumpfun-graduation-rate-analysis)). Bundle and cluster detection can be built in-house from the creation block's transactions plus a 1–3 hop funding walk, compared against Bubblemaps' 10%/15%/30% lines, and a persistent local blacklist of deployers and wallets seen in prior bundles or dumps is the cheapest durable control the arena can add.

For a Claude that shadows one top trader, the rules fall out of the copy-trading evidence, and they begin with choosing a leader the leaderboard would never surface. Require **30+ days of history and 50+ closed positions across many distinct tokens, transfer-filtered realized PnL positive on both 7-day and 30-day windows, a median hold measured in minutes to hours rather than seconds, a median first buy several minutes and many blocks after launch, no SOL funding links to deployers or to other first buyers of the same tokens, no synchronized buying with a fixed set of other wallets, not mirroring another wallet, and selective activity of a few tokens per day**, since 70–75% win-rate KOL wallets trade 3–5 tokens daily versus 15–20 for the churners ([WalletMaster](https://www.walletmaster.tools/blog/best-solana-copy-trading-tools/); [DeFade](https://defade.org/blog/solana-memecoin-insider-networks); [Solana Tracker](https://docs.solanatracker.io/guides/sniper-detection); [CoinStats/MadeOnSol](https://coinstats.app/news/07f38ebd47e8d820af3eb96b00c42a84b9b856c602626e3e4a71afbbd79014d6_KOL-Wallet-Tracking-on-Solana-What-the-Data-Actually-Shows-After-16-Million-Trades/)). Discount any win on a token where the leader's funding cluster held more than 10% of supply, since that is the bait-wallet signature. Re-validate weekly and drop the leader on a negative 7-day window or a style change (hold time collapsing, token count per day exploding), remembering that watched traders get worse: social traders whose follower counts changed performed about 10% worse in the following weeks ([UF News](https://news.ufl.edu/2025/03/crypto-social-traders/)). Per trade, admit a leader buy only if the token passes Tier 0–2, is older than a few minutes, has bundle or cluster supply under 20%, the leader's buy is not a small "probe" relative to their usual size (the KOL pattern of many small speculative buys most of which are never sold), and the agent's fill would land no more than a defined percentage above the leader's price; skip anything where the leader's own buy is a large share of the pool. Size as a fraction of the leader's size scaled to bankroll under a hard per-token cap, and treat the leader's first sell as a hard exit while also carrying your own take-profit, trailing stop and time stop, because the copier who waits for a "follow sell" to fill at the leader's price is the exit liquidity by construction ([Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/gmgn-explained)). On Fomo's manual timescale this means shadowing only traders whose edge is selection and patience rather than speed, which is also the only kind of edge a lagging follower can share.

Before any real money, the gate should be the three-stage, pre-registered evaluation the literature converged on. Freeze the strategy and prompt, replay it on the Parquet event history with the cost model from priority 1, then run it live on paper for at least four weeks, and only then fund it with an amount whose total loss is acceptable, expecting live fills to be materially worse than paper. Report every agent against three benchmarks over the identical window: SOL buy-and-hold, an equal-weight "buy every graduate that passes Tier 0 and hold N hours" baseline, and random entries with the same sizing. Publish the full set of statistics, not just return: alpha versus those benchmarks, maximum drawdown, trade count, fee drag as a percentage of equity, win rate with average win and average loss, and the share of total profit coming from the top three trades (if it is most of it, as in the 190-trade study, the result is not yet evidence of skill). Keep immutable decision logs and spot-check the model's stated reasons against the data snapshot it saw, since 57% of one deployed agent's sell rationales were invented. Treat anything under a few hundred trades as inconclusive; a two-week, six-model contest was judged too small to rank models even by its own organizers ([Protos](https://protos.com/llm-crypto-trading-contest-finds-llms-cant-trade-crypto/)). The practical bar for "this Claude is really good" is positive alpha against all three benchmarks over several hundred trades and two or more separate multi-week windows with the same frozen rules, with fee drag and profit concentration disclosed; an agent that merely loses less than the others is a good filter, which is valuable, but it is not yet a reason to deposit.

## Conclusion

The most useful reframing from this research is that the arena has been asking the wrong question. "Can a Claude pick the coin that runs?" has no evidence behind it anywhere in the literature, for humans, bots, or models, because the run is produced and harvested inside the creation block by participants with privileged position, and what is left for a minutes-later buyer is a negative-median, fat-tailed residual that fees usually consume. "Can a Claude reliably refuse the 75–98% of launches that are structurally built to dump, and then run a cheap, mechanical, event-exited strategy on the survivors?" is a question the evidence says is answerable, and the arena's own result (zero rugs, small positive) is the first data point that it can. The doubling target is best pursued not by making the agents bolder but by making the simulator more honest, the inputs structural, the exits mechanical, and the sample large enough that a +2.2% day stops being news.

The sober implication for real money is sequencing. The market's house edge is roughly $3 billion of extraction and a 5–8% round-trip toll on small tickets; the documented edges that beat it are speed, insider supply, or a few percent per trade from heavily filtered shadowing. A $1,000 account cannot buy speed or supply, so its only route is the filter plus patience, with sizing that survives forty consecutive losses and an evaluation that would catch a lucky streak before it was mistaken for skill. If an agent clears that bar on paper over hundreds of trades, a small live stake is a reasonable experiment; if it does not, no amount of narrative about AI or memecoins changes the arithmetic, and the honest expectation for the real-money phase is to learn what live slippage does to a paper edge, not to 10x.
