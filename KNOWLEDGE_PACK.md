## How a pump.fun token's life usually goes

1. **Launch.** The creator ("dev") launches a token on a **bonding curve**: a built-in price formula where every buy pushes the price up and every sell pushes it down. There's no separate liquidity pool yet.
2. **Early buyers.** Snipers and bots buy in the first seconds, often in the same block as the launch.
3. **Bonding progress.** As SOL flows in, the curve fills. Pump.fun shows this as a progress percentage. **King of the Hill** is the token closest to graduating at that moment, and it gets extra visibility.
4. **Graduation (migration).** When the curve completes (recent guides say around a $69k market cap; verify), liquidity moves to a regular pool (PumpSwap). The token now trades like any DEX token, and shows up on DexScreener and GeckoTerminal.
5. **After that:** most tokens fade within hours. A few keep running for days on a strong narrative or community.

**Most launches never graduate.** Most of those that do still fade. The runners are rare.

## Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Dev** | The wallet that created the token. |
| **Dev buy** | How much the dev bought at launch. Large = dev holds a big bag to dump. Small or none = less dump risk, but also less commitment. |
| **Dev sold / dev dumped** | The dev sold part or all of their bag. Often the start of the end, especially early. |
| **Bundle / bundled launch** | Several wallets (usually controlled by the dev or insiders) buy in the same block as the launch, to hide how much they own. Big red flag. |
| **Snipers** | Bots that buy in the first seconds and usually sell into the first wave of real buyers. |
| **Insiders** | Wallets connected to the dev, or with early access. They often hold a hidden share of the supply. |
| **Fresh wallets** | Newly created wallets with no history buying the token. Many fresh wallets usually means one person using many wallets. |
| **Top 10 holders %** | Share of supply held by the 10 biggest wallets, not counting the liquidity pool. High = a few people can crash it. |
| **Mint authority** | If NOT revoked, the dev can create more tokens at any time. |
| **Freeze authority** | If NOT revoked, the dev can freeze holders so they can't sell. |
| **LP burned / locked** | After graduation, whether the liquidity can be pulled. Burned or locked = can't be pulled. |
| **Rug / rug pull** | The dev or insiders pull liquidity or dump everything. The price goes to near zero in seconds. |
| **Slow rug** | The dev or insiders sell gradually over hours instead of all at once. |
| **Honeypot** | You can buy but can't sell (or selling is heavily taxed). |
| **Volume bots / wash trading** | Fake volume from bots trading with themselves, to look active and get onto trending lists. |
| **KOL** | "Key opinion leader": an influencer on X or Telegram. A **KOL call** is a post telling followers to buy. KOLs often buy before posting and sell into their followers. |
| **CTO** | "Community takeover": the dev abandoned the token and the community took over the socials and marketing. Sometimes revives a dead token. |
| **DEX paid / boosts** | Paid promotion on DexScreener (a profile, boosts, ads). It shows someone spent money, not that the token is good. |
| **Narrative / meta** | The current theme people are piling into: AI agents, a specific animal, a news event, a celebrity, politics. Metas rotate fast. |
| **Runner** | A token that keeps climbing well past launch, often 10x or more. |
| **Ape / aping in** | Buying fast without much research. |
| **Jeet** | Someone who sells quickly at a small profit. A token full of jeets struggles to keep climbing. |
| **Exit liquidity** | The late buyers that early holders sell to. If you're buying after a huge run with balanced buys and sells, you might be it. |
| **Dead cat bounce** | A short bounce after a big drop that fails and keeps falling. |
| **Second leg** | A second big run after the first pump and a pullback. Real runners often have one. |
| **ATH** | All-time high. |
| **Bag** | Your position in a token. "Bag holder" = stuck holding a token that crashed. |
| **Slippage** | The difference between the price you see and the price you actually get. Worse in thin pools and on fast-moving tokens. |
| **MEV / sandwich** | Bots that buy right before your buy and sell right after, so you pay more. |

## Good signs (usually)

- Buys clearly outnumber sells **and** the price rises steadily across 2, 5, 10 and 20 minutes, not just one spike.
- Many different buyers, with holder count growing steadily, not a few big wallets.
- Dev buy is small, and the dev isn't selling.
- No bundle at launch; top-10 holders under about 20–30% (excluding the pool).
- Socials attached and active: an X account, a Telegram, a website.
- Fits a narrative that's currently heating up.
- Volume is high relative to liquidity: an active, contested token.
- After graduation: deep liquidity (Fable found pools under about $40k cost 8–10% to trade in and out).
- **(test)** Tokens that survive their first 1–2 hours with steady volume are safer and slower.
- **(test)** A clean breakout to a new high after a pullback is a stronger entry than buying the first spike.

## Bad signs (usually)

- Dev sold, or is selling.
- Bundled launch, many fresh wallets, top-10 holding a large share.
- Mint or freeze authority NOT revoked.
- Huge 5-minute spike but the 2-minute change has turned negative: the spike is already fading.
- Huge 1-hour run with buys and sells now about equal: distribution, and you'd be exit liquidity.
- Buy *count* far higher than sell count, but the price isn't rising: probably bot spam (Fable's AGENCY lesson).
- 1-hour change deeply negative and a sudden bounce: usually a dead cat.
- Volume tiny compared with liquidity: nobody to sell to later.
- Only promoted (DEX paid / boosts), no organic interest.
- **(test)** KOL call already posted and the price already up a lot: late.

## Habits worth following

- Decide the stop and target **before** buying, not after it starts hurting.
- Scale out: sell 30–50% into strength and let the rest ride with a trailing stop.
- Don't re-buy a token that just stopped you out twice.
- When most of the top tokens are red on 5 and 10 minutes, the whole market is dumping. Wait in cash.
- Every trade has to beat its round-trip cost. In this arena that's currently about 7%, and much more in thin pools. A trade aiming for +10% in a thin pool isn't worth taking.
- Cash is a position. Sitting out a bad market is a win.
- A rug can gap straight past any stop. Size every position knowing it could go to zero.
