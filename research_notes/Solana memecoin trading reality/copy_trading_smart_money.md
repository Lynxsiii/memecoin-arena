# Copy Trading Top Wallets, "Smart Money" Tracking and KOL Calls on Solana Memecoins (2024-2026)

Scope note: Solana-focused, 2024-2026. The only peer-reviewed, Solana/Pump.fun-specific study of copy trading found is Luo et al. (ACM Web Conference 2026). Most other quantitative material comes from analytics vendors (Pine Analytics, MadeOnSol, Bubblemaps, Dune dashboards relayed by news sites) or from CEX copy-trading studies that are not Solana-specific. Where a figure comes only from a search snippet of a page that could not be fetched, or from an unattributed analyst, that is flagged. Tool-vendor guides (WalletMaster, MadeOnSol, Datawallet) are commercially motivated and are cited for mechanics, not for outcome claims.

Context for the arena: the user's mechanical copy bots lost 11-49% in one day. The evidence below is consistent with that: every rigorous source found says raw mirroring of leader trades is negative-expectancy after Solana frictions, and only heavily filtered shadowing (leader vetting + trade-level filtering + independent exits) shows positive copier returns, and then only a few percent per trade.

## KQ1. How copy-trading tools work on Solana (GMGN, Photon, BullX, Axiom, Cielo, Fomo, Trojan), and what latency/fees/slippage copiers face vs the original trader

### Takeaway
A copier is structurally 1-5 seconds (several Solana blocks) behind the leader, pays ~1% per side in platform fees (≈2% round trip) plus priority fees/tips plus 30-50% authorized slippage on new tokens, and buys after the leader's own buy has already moved the price on an illiquid token. The vendors themselves document that execution lag and per-order priority fees can turn a profitable leader into an unprofitable copy.

### Cited Findings
- Copy pipeline and delay: "Alpha wallet makes a trade, transaction confirms on Solana (~400ms block time), your tool detects the transaction, your copy trade is submitted and confirmed," with total delay "usually 1-5 seconds behind the original wallet"; "the lead wallet's purchase already moves price upward on low-liquidity tokens, so your entry point will be higher" — [MadeOnSol, Feb 26 2026](https://madeonsol.com/blog/best-solana-copy-trading-bots)
- Best-case latency claims: OdinBot claims "up to 65% of copy trades land in the same block"; top platforms claim execution "one block later" — [WalletMaster, Mar 9 2026](https://www.walletmaster.tools/blog/best-solana-copy-trading-tools/). Axiom markets sub-0.4s execution with colocated nodes; a reviewer notes "there is no universal latency ranking because execution outcomes depend on your environment, RPC setup and network state" — [telegramtrading.net GMGN vs Photon vs Axiom](https://telegramtrading.net/gmgn-vs-photon-vs-axiom/); [solanatradingbots.com Axiom vs Photon](https://solanatradingbots.com/compare/axiom-vs-photon/) (search-snippet level; vendor marketing claims, not independently measured)
- GMGN fees: "1% of the trade value" on both buys and sells, no volume discounts; round trip "total 2% before accounting for slippage, priority charges, and launchpad fees"; GMGN copy tasks follow up to ten wallets with per-task TP/SL — [Datawallet GMGN Explained, Sep 6 2026](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/gmgn-explained)
- GMGN's own documented copy-trading risks (as relayed by Datawallet): "Priority fees from frequent orders can turn a profitable target into an unprofitable copy"; copied orders "fill after the tracked wallet, potentially at significantly different prices"; "the system cannot determine in advance whether a tracked wallet intends to exit" — [Datawallet GMGN Explained](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/gmgn-explained)
- GMGN recommended settings circulating in guides: slippage 30-35% for automated orders, 50%+ for new tokens; priority fee 0.006-0.012 SOL (higher for competitive launches); Anti-MEV routes through protected nodes and on Solana requires a priority fee of at least 0.002 SOL — [coincodecap GMGN settings](https://coincodecap.com/best-settings-for-gmgn-bot); [cryptotrading-signals GMGN copy guide](https://cryptotrading-signals.beehiiv.com/p/how-to-copy-trade-profitably-on-gmgn) (search-snippet level, unofficial guides)
- Per-tool fee table (Mar 2026): Axiom 1% per trade tiered down to 0.75% at highest volume; Trojan 0.9-1%; BullX 0.9% (0.81% via referral); GMGN 1%; Photon 1% and "manual only, no copy trading"; Cielo free-$199/mo subscription (monitoring/alerts, execution via connected bot) — [WalletMaster](https://www.walletmaster.tools/blog/best-solana-copy-trading-tools/); Cielo tiers Free/10 Solana wallets, Pro $59/50, Whale $199/200 — [MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/best-solana-copy-trading-bots). Contradicted by one snippet claiming Axiom/BullX/Photon charge 0.5% and Trojan 1% — [rpcfast / solanatools fee comparison](https://solanatools.io/blog/solana-trading-bot-fees-compared) (fee schedules change; treat ~0.75-1% per side as the working figure)
- Fomo app (the arena's fee route) is "social trading rather than automated copy trading": following shows a trader's positions and sends alerts, "you still manually decide whether to respond"; spot fee "around 0.5% per trade"; gas sponsored; launched May 2025 by ex-dYdX staff; stated risks include "feed design encourages emotional trading" and "most launched tokens go to zero" — [Datawallet Fomo App Explained, Aug 17 2026](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/fomo-app-explained)
- Cost arithmetic from a trading-bot industry piece: "A trader buying $1,000 worth of a memecoin with 10% slippage might lose $50 to $100 per trade to MEV extraction"; bot fees of 0.5-1% mean "50 trades at $500 each with a 1% fee pays $250 in bot fees alone" — [quasa.io, Solana trading bot arms race](https://quasa.io/media/inside-the-solana-trading-bot-arms-race-who-wins-when-everyone-has-the-same-speed) (search-snippet only; page returned 404 on fetch; illustrative, not measured)
- Counterparty/security risk of custodial Telegram bots: TradeWiz_Bot incident drained "over 20,000 wallets," losses "$438,900" — [Coinfomania](https://coinfomania.com/solana-faces-critical-security-incident-as-20000-wallets) (search-snippet level)
- Structural problem in academic framing: Pump.fun's bonding curve is "strictly increasing and convex," so late entry is "mechanically penalized"; sniper bots buy "within 1-5 blocks (~0.4-2 seconds)" of launch, before copiers can react — [Luo, Feng, Xu, Liu, WWW 2026, arXiv 2601.08641](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v2)

### Inferences
- A copier's realistic hurdle per round trip on a fresh Pump.fun token is roughly: 2% platform fees + 0.01-0.03 SOL priority/tip per leg + whatever slippage actually fills (authorized 30-50%, realized often several %) + the leader's own price impact. On a 5-20k market-cap coin the leader's buy alone can be a double-digit % move, so the copier is frequently buying 10-30% above the leader's fill before the trade has "started."
- Because the leader's exit intent is unknowable, every copier is by construction the leader's exit liquidity on the sell side unless the copier exits first or independently.
- Fomo's manual "social trading" model means a Claude agent shadowing via Fomo is not competing on latency with automated copy bots; it is instead operating on the 10s-to-minutes timescale, which is only viable if it avoids the trades where speed is the whole edge (snipes and sub-minute flips).

### Gaps
- GMGN's official docs page for copy-trade settings (fixed-amount vs proportional buys, "follow sell", max buy, market-cap filters) could not be located via search; mechanics above are from third-party descriptions. Trojan's official 2026 copy-trading guide returned 403.
- No independent measurement of realized copier slippage or of same-block fill rates was found; all latency figures are vendor claims.

## KQ2. Evidence on copy-trading outcomes: do copiers profit? Front-running of copiers, bait wallets, leaderboards full of bots/insiders

### Takeaway
No source found shows unfiltered copiers profiting on Solana memecoins; the one peer-reviewed study finds that statistically selected "smart money" earns ~14% per trade while copiers of those same wallets earn nothing after frictions unless trades are heavily filtered (then ~3%). On-chain forensics show that a large share of the highest-win-rate wallets are deployer-funded snipers or bundlers whose 87% win rate and sub-minute exits are exactly what farms copiers, and that leaderboard PnL is inflated by unfiltered transfers/airdrops. CEX copy-trading data (not Solana) shows 97% of leaders profitable but fewer than half of followers profitable.

### Cited Findings
- Peer-reviewed Solana study (6,000 Pump.fun coins with wallet-level transaction and comment history): copy trading modeled "as a near zero-sum game where adversary profits directly correlate with copier losses." Identified smart-money wallets average "14% per trade"; after "realistic market frictions (including bonding curve mechanics and transaction fees)" copiers achieve "an estimated 3% positive return per trade" only with the authors' filtered multi-agent system; LASSO/XGBoost/NN wallet-selection baselines "achieve positive smart-money returns but fail to generate positive copier profitability once market frictions are applied" — [Luo, Feng, Xu, Liu, arXiv 2601.08641 (WWW 2026)](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v2)
- Same study's bot taxonomy and prevalence: bundle bots (creator buys across controlled wallets in the launch block) present in "approximately 25% of meme coin projects" and "associated with reduced returns and shorter dump duration"; sniper bots "appear in majority of projects"; bump bots (repetitive equal-size buy/sell cycles to fake volume) and comment bots ("moon incoming") are the attention-manipulation class. Removing the wallet-vetting agent cut copier returns 9.4% and AUC 11.3%, i.e. "wallet-level signals dominate decision-making" — [Luo et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v2)
- Deployer-funded sniping (Pine Analytics, Mar 15-Apr 21 2025): 15,000+ launches with same-block snipes, 4,600+ sniper wallets, 10,400+ deployers, ~1.75% of Pump.fun launches; "15,000+ SOL" realized profit in a month; "87% of snipes were profitable"; "55%+ exit within one minute," "85%+ exit within five minutes," "11%+ complete sales in 15 seconds or less"; the authors say leaderboards and copy platforms "may inadvertently amplify extractive behavior by highlighting wallets with high win rates" and "users following high-performers risk becoming exit liquidity for coordinated insiders"; advanced operators route SOL through "5-7 intermediary wallets" to defeat attribution — [Pine Analytics, Exit Liquidity Machines](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines)
- Copy-farming mechanics (practitioner guide; page returned 403, search-snippet only, anecdotal): a "profitable" wallet buys a 5k-20k market-cap coin, copiers auto-buy, "market cap goes to 100k-500k within 30 seconds, and the wallet immediately sells," taking "70%-90% of the money put into the trade"; red flags: "15-20% or more of a wallet's trades are sold within 5 seconds of buying," buy/sell under 60 seconds, under 2 minutes may be "a farmer, an MEV bot or a sniper bot" — [Medium, How To Find and Copy Trade Profitable Solana Wallets](https://medium.com/@mail_65621/how-to-find-and-copy-trade-profitable-solana-wallets-full-guide-848c5c3a53d0)
- Leaderboard PnL inflation: GMGN "does not document any filtering mechanism for transfers, airdrops, dust attacks, or MEV in its PnL calculations"; a wallet that sold airdropped tokens or wash-trades "looks more profitable than it is" — [WalletMaster, GMGN alternatives](https://www.walletmaster.tools/blog/best-gmgn-alternatives/) (vendor selling a competing tool); raw chain data "counts transfers between a trader's own wallets as profit"; also warns against "copying a wallet that's copying someone else" because "by the time the transaction reaches you, the price has already moved" — [WalletMaster](https://www.walletmaster.tools/blog/best-solana-copy-trading-tools/)
- GMGN user complaints enumerate "wash trading, hidden bundles, KOL/Smart wallet traders in cahoots, insiders, teams, liquidity rugs, price manipulation, and fresh wallet disguises"; GMGN's response is that it is "a data aggregator and trading tool, not a token issuer" — [Trustpilot GMGN reviews](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/gmgn.ai) (search-snippet level)
- CEX copy-trading outcomes (not Solana; perps on Binance/Bybit/MEXC; attributed to a YieldFund 2025 90-day study of 100,236 outcomes): blended follower win rate ~48.48%; "97% of lead traders recorded positive personal PnL, only 43.61% of them delivered positive returns to their followers"; Binance 66.50% follower win rate / +572,979 USDT, Bybit 43.65% / +346,106 USDT, MEXC 57.79% / -210,040 USDT; causes: followers "enter at worse prices," leverage/sizing mismatch, concentration, profit-share fees "10-30% on winnings" — [KuCoin blog relaying YieldFund study](https://www.kucoin.com/blog/is-crypto-copytrading-profitable-in-2026) (secondary source; original YieldFund document not located)
- Academic: Kawai, Soska, Routledge, Zetlin-Jones, Christin (CMU, CHI 2024), "Stranger Danger?", quantitative analysis of two crypto copy-trading platforms: "the platform UI significantly influences copiers' decisions, which is problematic because rankings are often easily gameable by unscrupulous leaders who prey on novice copiers and create perverse incentives" — [Kawai et al. CHI'24 PDF](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/nicolasc/publications/Kawai-CHI24.pdf) (abstract-level only; PDF text could not be extracted in this environment)
- Academic: Qiu (U. Florida) et al. 2025: on a crypto social-trading platform, traders whose follower counts changed (up or down) "performed about 10% worse in the weeks after," trading "more frequently" with "higher leverage"; gaining followers "creates an overconfidence effect" — [UF News, Mar 2025](https://news.ufl.edu/2025/03/crypto-social-traders/)
- Base rates for the population copiers are drawn from: Pump.fun, Aug 2024 (Dune, author unnamed): 60% of wallets lost money, 24% made under $100, 8.2% $100-1,000, 3% over $1,000, 0.5% over $10,000 — [Bitget News, Aug 21 2024](https://www.bitget.com/news/detail/12560604167959). March 2026: "roughly 49 to 50.6 percent of wallets trading Pump.fun tokens lost money, while another 45 percent made less than $500," i.e. ~96% lost or made under $500 — [CoinMarketCap Top Stories](https://coinmarketcap.com/top-stories/69cac98635bc806ad6e55590/) (snippet). 90-day Solana DEX sample (Aug 2026, analyst unattributed): 304,161 wallets, 6.25% (19,003) profitable, median loss $120, 88% of profitable wallets made under $100, only 25 wallets above $10,000, aggregate losses ~$1.26B — [Cointribune, Aug 20 2026](https://www.cointribune.com/en/crypto-solana-memecoins-trap-nearly-94-of-traders)
- Headline insider cases that top leaderboards would have surfaced as "smart money": Bubblemaps/Coffeezilla linked trader "Naseem" to >$100M TRUMP profit by buying immediately after launch — [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/cryptocurrency-news/bubblemaps-links-trader-naseem-to-100m-trump-memecoin-profit-93CH-3876530); LIBRA/MELANIA insider network, ~$87M withdrawn — [Yellow.com on Bubblemaps](https://yellow.com/news/bubblemaps-investigation-reveals-dollar100m-libra-token-scheme); $LAPTOP (Hunter Biden-promoted): "80% of traders lost money," 2 lost $100K-$1M, 100 lost $10K+, 700 lost $1K+, 11,000 smaller losses; $SI: 20% minted to dev plus 12% sniped by 8 wallets — [Bubblemaps blog](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/multimillion-dollar-theft-super-intelligence-suspected-insider-trades/)

### Inferences
- Win rate is the single most misleading leaderboard statistic in this market: the Pine Analytics sniper cohort (87% wins, sub-minute exits) and the user's own experience (mechanical copying of "top" wallets losing 11-49% in a day) are the same phenomenon viewed from both sides. A wallet that wins 85%+ of trades with median hold under 60 seconds is far more likely to be a sniper/farmer than a trader whose edge a lagging copier can share.
- The Luo et al. result is the best available quantification of the copier discount: ~14% leader edge collapses to ~0% for naive copiers and ~3% for filtered copiers. With ~2% round-trip fees plus priority fees, a 3% gross-per-trade expectation leaves little margin for the user's bot to be sloppy on slippage or sizing.
- CEX data is a useful floor, not a ceiling: on CEX perps there is no bonding curve, no 5k-mcap illiquidity and no same-block sniping, and followers still lose more often than not. Solana memecoin copying should be expected to be worse than that.

### Gaps
- No copy-trading platform (GMGN, Axiom, BullX, Trojan) publishes aggregate realized copier PnL; no independent on-chain study of copier wallets' returns on Solana was found. Figures on copier outcomes are either academic simulations (Luo et al.) or CEX perps data.
- The 304,161-wallet / 94%-lose study is unattributed in the press coverage; methodology (which DEXs, realized vs unrealized, bot exclusion) is unknown.
- The specific "farming" red-flag thresholds (15-20% of trades sold within 5s, etc.) are a single practitioner's heuristics, not validated.
- Kawai et al. (CHI'24) quantitative findings could not be extracted (PDF parsing unavailable); only the abstract-level claim is cited.

## KQ3. KOL/influencer calls: KOL wallets buying before posting and selling into followers; how followers fare

### Takeaway
On-chain investigators (ZachXBT, Bubblemaps) document the pattern of KOLs promoting bundled/insider-heavy low caps that then fall ~98%, and tracker data on ~1,000 KOL wallets shows median win rates near 57% with "most bought tokens go to zero and are never sold," plus thousands of coordinated multi-KOL buying clusters per month. No rigorous study quantifying followers' returns after a KOL post on Solana was found; evidence on follower outcomes is case-based.

### Cited Findings
- ZachXBT vs Ansem (Oct 5-7 2024): Ansem (507,000+ X followers) accused of promoting "hundreds" of low-cap Solana memecoins; named examples BODEN down 98.9% from a $1.04B ATH, HOBBES, ZEUS and WYNN each down ~98%; ZachXBT's thesis: "if you resort to promoting new low cap memecoins every few days to followers as a large account it's [because] you have no edge and have to use your followers instead"; Ansem's defense cited calling WIF at $100K market cap (later $4.8B) — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/zachxbt-slams-crypto-influencer-over-memecoin-promotions); [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/hacks/zachxbt-accuses-influencer-ansem-promoting-solana-memecoin-pump-dumps-ac9dfb13). The Cointelegraph piece "contains no specific data documenting actual losses suffered by Ansem's followers."
- Ansem's own token (June 2026): 67.38M $ANSEM airdropped to 704 wallets, "7 wallets received 74% of the tokens and quickly sold 38.29M $ANSEM for $1.29M"; token has "no official team, no roadmap, no utility" — [The Crypto Times, Jun 30 2026](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/06/30/ansems-9-43m-ansem-airdrop-7-wallets-got-74-already-dumping/) (search-snippet level)
- Bubblemaps found "a bundled memecoin was promoted by KOL James Wynn, with insiders holding 60% of the supply," and that he "has previously been involved with numerous memecoins where the supply was bundled and sold by insiders" — [Bubblemaps blog wrap-up](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/bubblemaps-wrap-up-seoul-scandals-memecoin-szn/) (search-snippet level)
- KOL wallet dataset (MadeOnSol, 1,058 tracked KOL wallets, 1.6M trades via validator gRPC, early 2026 onward): "Median win rate: 57.1%" (wallets with 5+ trades/24h), average 63.3%; top performers 70-75% achieved by trading "3-5 tokens per day instead of 15-20"; "volume and win rate are inversely correlated"; 30-day activity 495,720 buys vs 337,124 sells, avg buy 1.52 SOL, avg sell 2.24 SOL; "most bought tokens go to zero and are never sold"; 9,404 multi-KOL "cluster events" in 30 days, average 5.1 KOLs per cluster, average coordination score 67/100 — [MadeOnSol via CoinStats](https://coinstats.app/news/07f38ebd47e8d820af3eb96b00c42a84b9b856c602626e3e4a71afbbd79014d6_KOL-Wallet-Tracking-on-Solana-What-the-Data-Actually-Shows-After-16-Million-Trades/) (vendor data; win rate appears to count realized trades only)
- A Sep 29 2025 article claims KOL wallets posted "win rate exceeding 80%" over a week vs ~55% for smart-money addresses, but "cites 'Recent data from the Solana blockchain'" with no methodology, wallet count, or source — [Phemex](https://phemex.com/news/article/kol-wallets-outperform-smart-money-in-solana-meme-coin-market-22202) (low quality; unattributed)
- Hidden-promoter case studies from Bubblemaps: $LAPTOP (celebrity-promoted) "80% of traders lost money"; $SI dev minted 20% and 8 wallets sniped 12% — [Bubblemaps blog](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/multimillion-dollar-theft-super-intelligence-suspected-insider-trades/)
- Definition of the scam pattern: "undisclosed paid shilling where a creator is paid to hype a coin, with some receiving tokens at a discount and selling into the hype they created" — [Online Threat Alerts, Oct 2026](https://www.onlinethreatalerts.com/article/2026/10/2/crypto-influencer-scams-how-to-spot-a-fake-kol-promotion-and-protect-yourself) (general explainer)
- Social-trading incentive evidence (not memecoin-specific): traders with rising follower counts become more aggressive and perform ~10% worse — [UF News](https://news.ufl.edu/2025/03/crypto-social-traders/)

### Inferences
- The MadeOnSol numbers imply KOL "win rates" are flattering: with 1.5 buys per sell and most buys never sold, the ~57% median win rate is computed on the minority of positions that were closed, so true position-level hit rate is materially lower.
- The 9,404 cluster events/month (avg 5.1 KOLs buying the same token together) means a KOL buy is often part of a coordinated push rather than independent conviction; the followers who buy after the public post are structurally later than all 5 KOLs.
- Shadowing a KOL wallet differs from following KOL posts: the wallet buy precedes the post, so a wallet-shadower is ahead of the post-reading crowd but still behind the KOL and any co-cluster KOLs, and is the natural exit liquidity when the KOL sells "in larger chunks" (avg sell 2.24 SOL vs buy 1.52 SOL).

### Gaps
- No study found that measures returns to followers who buy after a KOL's public call on Solana (e.g., return at +1h/+24h after post, across many calls). The KOLSCAM tracking site (kolscams.fun) was unreachable.
- No on-chain analysis found that matches KOL wallet buy timestamps to their post timestamps at scale for 2024-2026; the "buy before post, sell into followers" pattern is documented by cases (Ansem, Wynn, LAPTOP, SI) rather than by a population statistic.

## KQ4. Which kind of wallet (if any) is worth shadowing; distinguishing genuine skill from insiders or luck using on-chain data

### Takeaway
The forensic literature converges on a checklist: a shadow-worthy wallet shows positive returns across many independent tokens over 30+ days with a statistically meaningful t-stat, hold times measured in minutes-to-hours (not seconds), no same-block/first-blocks entries, no funding links to deployers or to other buyers of the same tokens, no mirroring of other wallets, transfer-filtered PnL, and selective activity (few tokens per day). Sniper/bundler/insider wallets fail on hold time, entry timing and funding graph; lucky streaks fail on breadth and consistency.

### Cited Findings
- Luo et al.'s wallet agent "evaluates trader credibility using historical returns, t-statistics, consistency, and activity metrics," and the ablation shows wallet-level vetting is the dominant driver of copier returns; bot-presence features (bump-bot, sniper-bot flags) carry the highest feature importance in LASSO (0.2333 for bump-bot) — [Luo et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v2)
- Insider/sniper fingerprints (Pine Analytics): "direct SOL transfers between deployer and sniper pre-launch," "same-block transaction timing," "temporary wallet usage (one-time burners never used again)," "multi-signer transactions splitting initial purchases across 2-4 wallets," and time-of-day clustering 14:00-23:00 UTC; exits "55%+ within one minute," "90%+ use only one or two swap transactions" — [Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines)
- Insider-network fingerprints (DeFade, Feb 2026): "shared funding sources: multiple holders whose wallets were funded by the same address (or a small chain of addresses)"; "fresh wallet clusters... all created around the same time, all with similar initial SOL balances"; "temporal correlation: wallets that consistently buy and sell at the same times across multiple tokens"; "real traders don't operate in perfect synchronization"; post-sale convergence of proceeds to one collection wallet; 20%+ of supply in linked clusters is the elevated-risk threshold — [DeFade](https://defade.org/blog/solana-memecoin-insider-networks)
- Solana Tracker's sniper heuristics: "Balance = 0, profit > 0," "hold time < 5 minutes," "same wallet sniping many launches," "large buy size relative to pool"; bundlers = buying "in the same block as liquidity addition" or coordinated clusters; caveat that "timing and wallet labels help narrow a review; they do not establish a trader's intent" — [Solana Tracker docs](https://docs.solanatracker.io/guides/sniper-detection)
- Practitioner screening filters: "Win rate: minimum 60%," "Realized PnL: $5,000+ in last 30 days," "50+ trades," PnL verified "after transfer filtering," check "7-day, 30-day" windows not all-time, "reasonable holding periods (not bots)," use a "Copy Checker" to confirm the wallet "is generating original trades or mirroring someone else," and "copy 5-10 wallets with different trading strategies" — [WalletMaster](https://www.walletmaster.tools/blog/best-solana-copy-trading-tools/); "Look at 30+ day performance, not just the last 3 profitable trades"; "anyone can get lucky short-term" — [MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/best-solana-copy-trading-bots)
- Selectivity correlates with hit rate among KOL wallets: 70-75% win-rate wallets trade "3-5 tokens per day" vs 15-20 for high-frequency wallets — [MadeOnSol via CoinStats](https://coinstats.app/news/07f38ebd47e8d820af3eb96b00c42a84b9b856c602626e3e4a71afbbd79014d6_KOL-Wallet-Tracking-on-Solana-What-the-Data-Actually-Shows-After-16-Million-Trades/)
- Insider definition used by trackers: an insider wallet "consistently demonstrates 'perfect timing' — buying tokens seconds after deployment or accumulating large stakes before significant social media hype," distinguished from whales (size) and smart money (historical profitability) — [Bitget Wallet, insider wallets guide](https://web3.bitget.com/crypto-news/insider-wallets-solana-tracking-smart-money-and-on-chain-alpha) (search-snippet level)
- Nansen's Smart Money: "roughly 5,000-10,000 wallets" qualify at any time with labels such as "Smart Trader (consistent PnL)" and "90D Smart Trader"; the Solana guide gives "no explicit criteria" for win rate, holding time or airdrop adjustment and offers "no caveats" about copying — [Nansen Solana wallet guide, Apr 2025](https://nansen.ai/post/how-to-use-nansen-to-find-profitable-solana-wallet-addresses); [Eco explainer on Nansen](https://eco.com/support/en/articles/14800361-what-is-nansen-onchain-analytics-and-smart-money-explained)
- Population base rate against which "skill" must be judged: only 25 of 304,161 wallets made over $10,000 in a 90-day window, and 88% of the 6.25% that were profitable made under $100 — [Cointribune](https://www.cointribune.com/en/crypto-solana-memecoins-trap-nearly-94-of-traders) (unattributed analyst)
- Leader behavior degrades once watched: follower-count changes drive ~10% worse subsequent performance — [UF News](https://news.ufl.edu/2025/03/crypto-social-traders/)

### Inferences
- The wallets that are copyable at a 1-5 second (or Fomo-manual) lag are precisely those whose edge is not timing: traders who buy after a token has graduated/established liquidity and hold for minutes-to-hours, with many independent winners. Those wallets will look worse on leaderboards (lower win rate, lower 24h PnL) than snipers, so a leaderboard-sorted search selects against them.
- A practical "insider vs skill" triage from the sources: (1) first-buy position and blocks-since-launch across the wallet's history (median > a few minutes after launch is good; frequent top-10 first buyer is bad); (2) median hold time (> several minutes); (3) funding graph (no SOL from deployers/first buyers; wallet age > weeks; not a fresh burner); (4) breadth (dozens of distinct tokens with positive expectancy over 30+ days, not two 50x hits); (5) transfer-filtered realized PnL; (6) not mirroring another wallet; (7) no synchronized buys with a fixed set of other wallets.
- Luckiness: a wallet with a handful of trades and one 100x is indistinguishable from luck; the t-stat criterion in Luo et al. formalizes this. Given the base rate (25 wallets above $10k among 304k), prior probability that any given top-PnL wallet is an insider or bot is high.

### Gaps
- No source provides validated numeric thresholds (e.g., minimum t-stat, minimum hold time, minimum number of tokens) that separate skill from luck with measured out-of-sample accuracy; Luo et al. use such features but the paper summary does not give cutoffs.
- Nansen's Smart Money labeling criteria are proprietary and undisclosed; no evidence found on how Smart-Money-labeled Solana wallets perform when copied.

## KQ5. Practical rules for a shadowing agent: which trades to copy, sizing relative to the leader, when to exit independently

### Takeaway
The evidence supports shadowing only as a filtered, independently-managed strategy: copy only leader buys that are not snipes/sub-minute flips and that occur in tokens with enough liquidity to absorb a lagged entry; size small and capped per token with a hard daily loss budget; never rely on the leader's sell for the exit; treat the leader's sell as a hard signal to leave but also pre-set your own TP/SL; and re-validate the leader weekly on 7d/30d transfer-filtered data. Expect at best a few percent per trade gross (Luo et al.), so fee and slippage discipline decides the sign of the result.

### Cited Findings
- Trade-level filtering is what turns negative copier returns positive in the only rigorous study: the coin agent "detects manipulative bot presence" (bundle/sniper/bump/comment bots) and the timing agent "evaluates entry timing based on purchase price, quantity, and dollar amount"; combined system yields ~3%/trade for copiers vs failure for unfiltered statistical selection — [Luo et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v2)
- Trades to avoid: leader buys in the same block as deployment or within the first blocks (sniper/bundler behavior), and leaders who exit "within one minute" in 55%+ of trades — [Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines); trades in tokens with bundle bots (present in ~25% of projects, "reduced returns and shorter dump duration") — [Luo et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v2); tokens where linked clusters hold 20%+ of supply — [DeFade](https://defade.org/blog/solana-memecoin-insider-networks)
- Sizing and limits recommended by tool guides: "Cap how much you invest in any single token"; "Follow 5-10 wallets with smaller amounts each"; "Set stop losses"; use blacklists to "Ignore trades in tokens you don't want"; "Check your copy trading results weekly" — [MadeOnSol](https://madeonsol.com/blog/best-solana-copy-trading-bots); cap per-wallet exposure and diversify across strategies — [WalletMaster](https://www.walletmaster.tools/blog/best-solana-copy-trading-tools/)
- Exits cannot be delegated: GMGN documentation states "the system cannot determine in advance whether a tracked wallet intends to exit," and copy tasks allow custom TP/SL precisely because copied sells fill later and worse — [Datawallet GMGN Explained](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/gmgn-explained)
- Order frequency is a cost center: "Priority fees from frequent orders can turn a profitable target into an unprofitable copy" — [Datawallet GMGN Explained](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/gmgn-explained); 2% round-trip fee plus 30-50% authorized slippage "can render an apparently successful trade unprofitable after costs" — same source
- Leaders sell bigger than they buy: KOL avg sell 2.24 SOL vs avg buy 1.52 SOL; "small speculative buys across many tokens, then larger exits on the ones that work" — [MadeOnSol via CoinStats](https://coinstats.app/news/07f38ebd47e8d820af3eb96b00c42a84b9b856c602626e3e4a71afbbd79014d6_KOL-Wallet-Tracking-on-Solana-What-the-Data-Actually-Shows-After-16-Million-Trades/)
- Follower-side causes of the leader/follower gap (CEX study, generalizable): worse entry prices from lag, "leverage and sizing mismatches," over-concentration in one leader, fee drag — [KuCoin relaying YieldFund](https://www.kucoin.com/blog/is-crypto-copytrading-profitable-in-2026); [Stax Investing](https://staxinvesting.com/blog/is-copy-trading-profitable-the-honest-risk-picture)
- Don't copy copiers: "copying a wallet that's copying someone else" guarantees a stale entry — [WalletMaster](https://www.walletmaster.tools/blog/best-solana-copy-trading-tools/)
- Fomo-specific: following is alert-plus-manual, ~0.5% fee, gas sponsored; the vendor's own listed risk is that the feed "encourages emotional trading" — [Datawallet Fomo](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/fomo-app-explained)

### Inferences (rules a Claude shadowing agent can adopt; derived from the cited findings, not from a tested system)
- Leader selection (re-run weekly): require 30+ days of history, 50+ closed positions across many distinct tokens, transfer-filtered realized PnL positive on both 7d and 30d, median hold time of at least several minutes, median entry at least several minutes/blocks after launch, no deployer-linked funding, no synchronized co-buyers, not a mirror of another wallet. Drop the leader on any week with negative 7d PnL or a change in style (hold time collapsing, token count/day exploding).
- Trade admission (per leader buy): skip if the token is under a liquidity/market-cap floor where the leader's own buy is a large share of the pool; skip if the leader is among the first buyers or the token is under N minutes old; skip if bundle/cluster share of supply is above ~20%; skip if the leader's buy is a small "probe" relative to their typical size (KOL pattern: many small speculative buys, most never sold); only act when the agent can fill within its latency window at a price not more than a defined % above the leader's fill.
- Sizing: size as a fraction of the leader's size scaled to the agent's bankroll, with a hard per-token cap and a per-day loss budget set below the 11-49% one-day drawdowns already observed; Luo et al.'s ~3%/trade expectation means a single 30-50% slippage fill or a dumped-on-copiers exit wipes out ten good trades, so the cap must be small.
- Exits: pre-set TP/SL at entry; treat the leader's first sell as a hard exit trigger (leaders sell bigger than they buy and the copier is otherwise the exit liquidity); exit on time stop if the leader has not sold within the leader's typical hold window; never wait for a "follow sell" to fill at the leader's price.
- Costs: minimize order count (no multi-leg scaling in/out), set slippage per-token rather than a blanket 50%, and account for Fomo's ~0.5% per side plus realized slippage when judging whether a leader's historical per-trade edge (which is computed at the leader's fills) survives for a lagged copier.

### Gaps
- No published backtest or live track record of any rule set like the above on Solana was found other than Luo et al.'s simulated ~3%/trade; the exact filters and thresholds that produced it are described qualitatively in the paper summary, not as reproducible parameters.
- No evidence on what fraction of a leader's position a copier should take (sizing ratio); all sizing guidance found is qualitative ("cap," "small amounts").
- No data on the optimal independent exit rule (fixed TP/SL vs time stop vs leader-sell trigger) for lagged copiers on Solana.
