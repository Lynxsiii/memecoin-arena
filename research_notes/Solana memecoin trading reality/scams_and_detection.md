# Scams and Adversarial Behavior in Solana Memecoins, and On-Chain Detection (2024-2026) — Defender's Catalogue

Scope: how memecoin buyers on pump.fun / PumpSwap / Raydium lose money to other actors, how common each pattern is, and what concrete on-chain checks an AI trading agent can run before buying. Defensive focus only. Research conducted 2026-10-06; sources span 2024 to mid-2026.

Note on source quality: the strongest quantitative sources found are (a) the Solidus Labs 2025 Rug Pull Report, (b) a 2026 academic preprint on H1-2025 Solana DEX rug pulls (arXiv 2603.24625), (c) Pine Analytics' deployer-funded sniper study, (d) the Ghostlogs/sandwiched.me MEV dataset presented at Accelerate 2025, (e) the Helius Solana MEV report on DeezNode, and (f) the Luo et al. copy-trading paper (arXiv 2601.08641). Several widely repeated figures (e.g., "60-80% of pump.fun volume is bots", "93% of Solana activity is non-organic") could not be traced to a verifiable primary source and are listed under Gaps.

## Q1. How common are rug pulls and soft rugs on pump.fun / Raydium / PumpSwap, and what are the typical mechanics?

### Takeaway
By every quantitative study found, the overwhelming majority of Solana memecoin launches end as pump-and-dumps or liquidity withdrawals: Solidus Labs found ~98.6% of 7M+ pump.fun tokens (Jan 2024-Mar 2025) failed to retain $1k of liquidity and ~93% of 388k Raydium pools showed soft-rug characteristics, and an independent 2026 preprint flagged 76% of 100k new tokens on Orca/Raydium/Meteora in H1 2025 as rug-pull candidates. The dominant 2025-2026 mechanic is not a technical exploit but coordinated dev/insider wallet dumping (pump-and-dump), followed by liquidity withdrawal; mint/freeze-authority abuse is comparatively rare but catastrophic.

### Cited Findings
- Solidus Labs analyzed 7M+ pump.fun tokens with at least five trades (Jan 2024-Mar 2025); only ~97,000 maintained liquidity above $1,000, i.e., "98.6% of tokens on Pump.fun collapse into worthless pump-and-dump schemes" shortly after launch — [Solidus Labs 2025 Rug Pull Report](https://www.soliduslabs.com/reports/solana-rug-pulls-pump-dumps-crypto-compliance); summarized by [Forklog](https://forklog.com/en/report-98-of-pump-funs-memecoins-deemed-scams/) and [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/defi/alarming-99-of-memecoin-launches-on-pumpfun-are-pump-and-dumps-or-rug-pulls-report)
- Solidus examined ~388,000 Raydium pools; ~93% (~361,000) exhibited "soft rug pull" characteristics, defined as creators funding a pool, waiting for trader activity, then removing ~90% of liquidity. Loss distribution: 25% of rug pulls involved under $732, median ~$2,832, largest single rug $1.9M — [Solidus Labs](https://www.soliduslabs.com/reports/solana-rug-pulls-pump-dumps-crypto-compliance)
- Solidus definitions: "Rug Pull" = abrupt liquidity withdrawal by creators; "Soft Rug Pull" = sudden withdrawal of liquidity from Raydium pools; "Pump-and-Dump" = coordinated manipulation where early buyers profit as price inflates, then crashes when creators liquidate — [Solidus Labs](https://www.soliduslabs.com/reports/solana-rug-pulls-pump-dumps-crypto-compliance)
- Solidus notes pump.fun's bonding curve "increases exponentially per purchase, benefiting early buyers and creators while disadvantaging late entrants" — [Solidus Labs](https://www.soliduslabs.com/reports/solana-rug-pulls-pump-dumps-crypto-compliance)
- A 2026 academic preprint (arXiv 2603.24625v2) detected 76,469 candidate rug-pull tokens among 100,063 newly issued tokens on Orca, Raydium and Meteora between Jan 1 and Jun 30, 2025. Composition: 60,402 pump-and-dump candidates; 15,606 liquidity-withdrawal candidates; 461 freeze-authority-abuse candidates. Directly traceable losses: "at least $151,089,437.80 across 7,322 profitable addresses" in SOL/USDC/USDT. Random audit found a 0.26% false-positive rate (95% upper bound 1.45%) — [DeepStrike rug-pull statistics roundup, citing arxiv.org/html/2603.24625v2](https://deepstrike.io/blog/rug-pull-statistics)
- Serial deployer analysis of pump.fun (as of April 2025): 2.43M+ tokens created; daily deployments often exceed 16,000; only 1.4% graduate to Raydium; fewer than 0.002% sustain a $1M market cap; top 1% of deployers account for nearly 30% of all launches; ~15% of deployers manage clusters of 50+ wallets showing "simultaneous token launches or synchronized liquidity withdrawals"; some serial deployers reportedly earn "over $60,000 per week by rugging 98% of memecoins" — [Killy7, Serial Token Deployers on Solana](https://killy7.substack.com/p/serial-token-deployers-on-solana)
- Serial-deployer red flags listed: large sell-offs following price pumps; liquidity withdrawals after bonding-curve milestones; frequent deployments from a single wallet; synchronized trading across multiple addresses — [Killy7](https://killy7.substack.com/p/serial-token-deployers-on-solana)
- Across 34,988 meme coins on Ethereum, BNB, Solana and Base (three-month longitudinal study), 82.8% of tokens with >100% gains "demonstrated evidence of artificial growth strategies" (wash trading, liquidity-pool-based price inflation, pump-and-dump/rug pull); 17,000+ victimized addresses with losses above $9.3M — [arXiv 2507.01963, "A Midsummer Meme's Dream"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01963)
- Post-graduation soft-rug signature described by a Chinese-language analysis of pump.fun/PumpSwap: when launchpad volume is artificially inflated, tokens "graduate based on fake curve activity, and there are no real buyers when they enter PumpSwap", so the normal relationship between launchpad and PumpSwap volume breaks — [TechFlow](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/article/30905) (secondary; not independently verified)

### Inferences
- "98.6% are scams" overstates what Solidus measured: the metric is failure to retain $1k of liquidity, which conflates abandoned/failed tokens with deliberate fraud. The more conservative preprint figure (76% rug-pull candidates with explicit P&D / LP-withdrawal / freeze-abuse labels and an audited 0.26% FP rate) is a better base rate for "tokens that will be deliberately dumped on you."
- For an AI agent, the practical prior is: any freshly launched pump.fun token should be assumed a dump-in-progress unless on-chain checks say otherwise. Base rates of 75-98% mean a single "clean" scanner score is far too weak to overturn the prior.
- The dominant loss mechanism in 2025-2026 is pump-and-dump by the deployer's own wallet cluster (60k of 76k flagged tokens in the preprint), not LP pulls (15.6k) and not authority abuse (461). Detection effort should therefore be weighted toward holder-cluster and deployer-behavior analysis rather than only authority flags.
- On pump.fun, LP cannot be "pulled" during the bonding-curve phase (the curve holds the SOL); the LP-pull risk re-emerges after graduation to PumpSwap/Raydium if the creator controls LP tokens. The Solidus Raydium figures (93% of pools) apply to Raydium V4 pools generally, which includes but is not limited to graduated pump.fun tokens.
- Median losses of ~$2.8k per Raydium soft rug imply most rugs are small, high-frequency operations by serial deployers; the long tail ($1.9M) comes from tokens that attracted real retail attention.

### Gaps
- No source gave a separate soft-rug rate for PumpSwap pools specifically (post March-2025 launch); Solidus' pool data is Raydium V4.
- Could not retrieve the full title/author list of arXiv 2603.24625; figures are via DeepStrike's summary of the v2 HTML.
- No Chainalysis 2025 or 2026 Crypto Crime Report section specific to Solana memecoin rug pulls was found; search results surfaced only non-rug categories (e.g., wrench attacks). Chainalysis' last widely cited rug-pull figure ($2.8B) dates to 2021.

## Q2. Mint/freeze authority abuse and Token-2022 extension traps (honeypots)

### Takeaway
On Solana, the "honeypot" levers are readable straight off the mint account rather than hidden in contract bytecode: an active freeze authority (freeze buyers' token accounts so they cannot sell), an active mint authority (inflate supply), and Token-2022 extensions — permanent delegate (burn/transfer tokens out of any holder's wallet), transfer fees (hidden tax), transfer hooks (programmable sell blocking) and non-transferable. These are rare relative to dumps (461 freeze-abuse candidates out of 76k flagged tokens in H1 2025) but are total-loss events, and they are cheap to check.

### Cited Findings
- A freeze authority lets its holder freeze any token account; frozen tokens "can't sell, can't transfer, can't do anything." Malicious creators "allow buying to pump the price, then either freeze all holders and demand ransom, or selectively freeze everyone except their own wallets and dump" — [dev.to, "Freeze authority is the Solana honeypot"](https://dev.to/mrvlyouknowwho/freeze-authority-is-the-solana-honeypot-how-to-check-any-spl-token-in-10-seconds-free-no-wallet-15h4)
- On Solana a honeypot (buyable but not sellable) "can be achieved through frozen accounts, transfer hooks, or excessively high transfer fees" — [MadeOnSol, Honeypot](https://madeonsol.com/learn/honeypot)
- Victims of the frozen-account variant see the swap fail on Solscan with "Instruction #4 Failed - Account is frozen" — [GMGN, "Watch out honeypot methods on Solana rug pull tokens"](https://gmgnai.notion.site/Watch-out-honeypot-methods-on-Solana-rug-pull-tokens-Instruction-4-Failed-Account-is-frozen-error-78e167f3343b4dfe98a216c86b8ecdf0) (page content could not be fetched; claim from search snippet)
- A scanner heuristic: if a freeze authority exists, check the top 20 token accounts and report any in a frozen state; "multiple frozen top accounts being a strong honeypot signal" — [Apify Solana Token Safety Checker](https://apify.com/sanjeep/solana-token-safety-checker?fpr=data)
- Token-2022 "Permanent Delegate" grants "unrestricted delegate privileges over all Token Accounts for that mint, enabling them to burn or transfer tokens without limitation." Scammers used it to burn buyers' tokens; one victim's tokens were burned within seven seconds of their swap. First publicly reported by Slorg (Jupiter Core Working Group) on Sept 3, 2024; also analyzed by PeckShield and Beosin. Motivations cited: reduce float to hold price artificially, extract small amounts ($50+) across many launches. Jupiter and RugCheck added indicators for the extension — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/new-solana-scam-burn-tokens-from-inside-sol-wallets)
- Solana's own docs call permanent delegate a "double-edged sword" that can be abused — [Solana docs, Permanent Delegate](https://solana.com/docs/tokens/extensions/permanent-delegate)
- Raydium blocks the Permanent Delegate extension for permissionless pool creation because it "allows unlimited burn/transfer authority over any holder's tokens" — [Raydium docs, Token-2022 support](https://docs.raydium.io/raydium/build/resources/token-2022-support) (page returned 404 on direct fetch; claim from search snippet)
- GoPlus's Solana Token Security API exposes `freezable.status`, `mintable.status`, `balance_mutable_authority`, `closable`, `transfer_fee`, `transfer_hook`, holder concentration, and DEX liquidity for SPL and SPL-2022 tokens; keyless endpoint `https://api.gopluslabs.io/api/v1/solana/token_security?contract_addresses=<MINT>` — [GoPlus docs](https://docs.gopluslabs.io/); [QuickNode guide](https://www.quicknode.com/fr/builders-guide/tools/goplus-security-by-goplus-labs)
- The mint account can be decoded directly for mint authority, freeze authority and Token-2022 extension bytes without simulating a sell — [dev.to, programmatic SPL token risk analysis](https://dev.to/verixia_233e4721792ce3390/programmatic-spl-token-risk-analysis-inspecting-mint-freeze-and-token-2022-extension-bytes-l0d)
- In the H1-2025 preprint, 461 of 76,469 flagged tokens were freeze-authority-abuse candidates — [DeepStrike citing arXiv 2603.24625v2](https://deepstrike.io/blog/rug-pull-statistics)

### Inferences
- Standard pump.fun launches (SPL token, mint and freeze authority revoked by the program) are structurally immune to freeze/mint/permanent-delegate honeypots; these traps appear mainly in tokens created outside pump.fun or via launchpads that allow custom mints. An agent should treat `token program == Token-2022` as a hard-stop for review and any non-null freeze or mint authority as an automatic reject for memecoins.
- Because these checks are a single `getAccountInfo` on the mint plus a scan of top holder accounts for the `frozen` state, they should run on every candidate before any other analysis; they are cheap and near-deterministic.
- Transfer-fee and transfer-hook extensions are not inherently malicious, but for a memecoin they have no legitimate purpose and should be rejected by default.

### Gaps
- No prevalence estimate found for transfer-fee or transfer-hook honeypots specifically (only freeze-authority abuse was counted in the preprint).
- Could not fetch Raydium's exact list of blocked vs. allowed Token-2022 extensions (404).

## Q3. How do bundlers, snipers and insider wallet clusters work, and how are they detected?

### Takeaway
Bundling (many deployer-controlled wallets buying in the launch block, often atomically) and deployer-funded sniping are the main way insiders secure cheap supply to dump on later buyers; Pine Analytics found 15k+ pump.fun tokens where a same-block sniper had a direct SOL-transfer link to the deployer, with 55% of those positions exited within one minute. Detection is well established: same-block/first-N-block buys, atomic multi-wallet transactions, shared funding ancestry, tight buy-size ranges, later consolidation transfers, and cross-launch recurrence of the same wallets; Bubblemaps and Mobula publish thresholds (bundled supply >15% serious, >30% avoid).

### Cited Findings
- Definition: bundling is "a coordinated buy where multiple wallets purchase the token in the same block or within the first few blocks of trading, before any public buyer has had a chance to enter," so one entity accumulates supply at the lowest price while public buyers chase momentum — [Bubblemaps blog](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/how-to-analyze-meme-coin-holders-with-bubblemaps/)
- Bubblemaps bundled-supply thresholds: <5% low concern (normal sniping); 5-15% moderate, track the wallets; >15% serious (insiders have a large cost advantage); >30% "effectively pre-bought by insiders before the public had access ... avoid" — [Bubblemaps blog](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/how-to-analyze-meme-coin-holders-with-bubblemaps/)
- Bubblemaps cluster-concentration thresholds (supply held by wallets linked to a single entity): <15% reasonably healthy; 15-30% elevated; 30-50% high; >50% "extreme risk" (single-entity control). Deployer red flags: retained tokens post-launch, supply distributed to a network of addresses before coordinated dumps, launch-day wallet creation, no prior on-chain history. Healthy signs: multiple unconnected clusters, CEX/DEX wallets among top holders, deployer near zero balance, declining top-holder concentration. Analysis is most useful "during first 72 hours." Solana's low fees let one deployer manage "30 to 50 wallets simultaneously" — [Bubblemaps blog](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/how-to-analyze-meme-coin-holders-with-bubblemaps/)
- Bundle vs. cluster: a bundle is "a set of wallets that transacted together during the token launch: typically all buying in the same block or within the same snipe window. The link is temporal and transactional." A cluster is "a set of wallets linked by on-chain behavior over time: shared funding sources, coordinated transfers, or recycled addresses. The link is structural and behavioral." Cluster signals: shared funding source, cross-token recurrence of the same wallet group, internal transfers before exchange sales. "A bundle holding 10%+ of total supply is a major red flag"; example of 30 wallets collectively holding 19% of supply — [Bubblemaps, Bundle vs Cluster](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/whats-the-difference-between-bundle-cluster-2/)
- Mobula sniper signals: purchase within 0-3 blocks of creation; same block as deployment; recently created wallet; funded just before launch; high priority fee or MEV-bundle participation. Bundler signals (cluster of 3+ wallets): multiple distinct wallets in one atomic transaction; all funded from the same source; identical block; buy amounts in a tight range; later consolidation to one wallet. API fields: per-holder `labels` (sniper/bundler/insider), `firstTradeAt`; token-level `snipersHoldingsPercentage`, `bundlersHoldingsPercentage`. Caveat: "1-2 snipers in top 20 (common, not always bad)" is only a yellow flag — [Mobula docs, Detecting snipers & bundlers](https://docs.mobula.io/almanac/detecting-snipers-bundlers)
- Pine Analytics identified 15,000+ pump.fun tokens (from Mar 15, ~1.75% of launch activity in the window) where the launch was sniped in the same block by a wallet that had a direct SOL transfer with the deployer before launch; 4,600+ sniper wallets, 10,400+ deployers. Exit timing: 11%+ fully exited within 15 seconds, 55%+ within one minute, 85% within five minutes; 90%+ of snipers sell in one or two swaps. Realized profit 15,000+ SOL over one month with an 87% win rate; "most profitable group by far is wallets that exit in under one minute." Method limitation: only direct funding links were traced, missing 5-7-hop obfuscation chains — [Pine Analytics, Exit Liquidity Machines](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines)
- Pine Analytics' recommended defenses: surface top-wallet concentration and exit patterns in the first 20-50 blocks; persistent high-risk labels for repeat offenders; behavioral trust scoring that rewards wallets with demonstrable cost (age, capital, activity depth) — [Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines)
- Funding ancestry matters more than wallet age: "a fresh wallet that received funds from an unrelated exchange deposit is different from several fresh wallets funded by one controller shortly before coordinated buys" — [Bubblemaps, Bundle vs Cluster](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/whats-the-difference-between-bundle-cluster-2/)
- Luo et al. (Pump.fun, 6,000 projects): "bundle bots" and "gradual bundles" split accumulated positions across many controlled wallets to hide concentration from copiers; sniper bots buy within 1-5 blocks (~0.4-2 s) of creation; bundle bots appear in ~25% of projects and sniper bots in the majority; bundle-bot presence is associated with reduced maximum returns and shorter profitability windows. Formal heuristic: bundle/sniper = same-block or first-5-block non-creator purchases at launch — [arXiv 2601.08641](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v2)
- A 2026 preprint on pump.fun (1.58M buyer observations, 166k launches, Jun 11-25 2026) used union-find on cross-launch co-occurrence graphs to surface 1,012 persistent wallet cohorts of 2-12 wallets, but the authors withdrew the numerical results after finding data-quality errors (buyer records included sells and were missing buys). Detection code and cohort data were released under CC-BY-4.0 — [arXiv 2607.02795](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02795)
- Case study of cross-launch insider clustering: Bubblemaps (with Coffeezilla) linked wallet "0xcEA" to both MELANIA (sniped on Jan 19 2025 for ~$2.4M profit, funds moved to Avalanche through obfuscated multi-wallet transfers) and LIBRA (funded creation; sniped on Feb 15 2025 for ~$6M via side wallets funded cross-chain from Arbitrum/Avalanche). Method: "analyzing cross-chain transfers and timing patterns" — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/341238/bubblemaps-links-single-entity-to-libra-and-melania-memecoins)
- Holder-concentration rules of thumb used by scanner guides: top holder <5% normal, 5-10% note, >10% significant, >20% very high; top-10 holders <40% excluding LP/burn; no single wallet >10%; creator <5% — [Solana Tracker token-safety guide](https://docs.solanatracker.io/guides/token-safety.md); [MadeOnSol RugCheck guide](https://madeonsol.com/blog/how-to-use-rugcheck)

### Inferences
- An agent can implement a first-pass bundle detector with only transaction history for the launch block(s): (1) list all buys in the creation block and the next ~5 blocks; (2) group buys that share a transaction signature (atomic bundle) or a Jito bundle; (3) for each buyer, walk SOL funding back 1-3 hops and group wallets sharing a funder; (4) sum the share of current supply held by each group. Compare against Bubblemaps' 15%/30% lines and the 10% "single bundle" line.
- Because Pine Analytics only caught direct deployer-to-sniper transfers (and still found 15k tokens), the true bundling rate is materially higher than 1.75%; Luo et al.'s ~25% bundle-bot prevalence is a more realistic working estimate for pump.fun.
- The exit-timing data (55% out within 60 s) means a buyer entering more than a minute after launch is usually buying from, not alongside, insiders. The practical defense for an agent is to never be the counterparty to a same-block cluster: either skip tokens with >10-15% bundled supply or wait until the bundle has demonstrably exited (sold) before entering.
- Cross-launch recurrence (same wallet set appearing across many launches) is the highest-precision insider signal because it cannot be faked cheaply; maintaining a local blacklist of wallets observed in prior bundles/dumps is an inexpensive and durable control.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed estimate of the share of pump.fun launches that are bundled by the deployer (the one 2026 preprint attempting this withdrew its numbers).
- No public benchmark of precision/recall for Bubblemaps' or Mobula's bundler labels.

## Q4. Wash trading, fake volume and bot activity: prevalence and detection signals

### Takeaway
Bot-generated activity dominates pump.fun/PumpSwap flow: an on-chain analyst found 93 of the top 100 wallets by activity are bots, and academic work finds wash-trading campaigns on Solana memecoins begin a median 62 days after creation and persist ~2-3 months. Detection signals are matched buy/sell pairs with little net position change ("bump bots"), volume concentrated in a few wallets active 18+ hours/day, and launchpad volume that does not carry over to PumpSwap after graduation.

### Cited Findings
- On-chain researcher Adam (@Adam_Tehc) found "93 out of the top 100 pumpfun/pumpswap wallets are bots"; the top 25 wallets generated $100M-$494M in volume each and were all bots; after filtering, only two human wallets exceeded $100M volume, 785 (0.005%) exceeded $10M, 11,843 (0.078%) exceeded $1M. Bot tell: "These top wallets often stay active for more than 18 hours a day." Filtering methodology not disclosed — [Crypto Times, Jun 10 2025](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2025/06/10/93-out-of-top-100-wallets-on-pump-fun-are-bots-crypto-sleuth/); [Cryptopolitan](https://www.cryptopolitan.com/active-wallets-on-pump-fun-automate-trades/)
- Across 34,988 meme coins (ETH/BNB/Solana/Base), most tokens experience their first manipulation at a median of 85 days, with Solana tokens targeted earlier at 62 days; wash-trading campaigns average 87 days (median 64); ~19.9% of wash-trading events occur within one week of launch and 49.4% within one month — [arXiv 2507.01963](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01963)
- Luo et al. define "bump bots" that "execute matched buy-sell pairs to inflate trading volume and visibility" and "comment bots" that generate hype; detection heuristic for bump bots is a high ratio of matched buy-sell pairs relative to net position change; comment bots are classified via LLM as contextless, slogan-like hype. Attention-manipulation bots correlate with superior reported performance metrics — [arXiv 2601.08641](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v2)
- A TUM master's thesis (Wiedenmann, 2025) on wash trading in Solana's high-throughput, low-fee environment reports "a substantial presence of wash trading activity, concentrated among a small set of accounts and tokens" — [TUM thesis PDF](https://www.cs.cit.tum.de/fileadmin/w00cfj/sebis/thesis/250610_Wiedenmann_Thesis.pdf) (from search snippet; full text not fetched)
- Pump.fun deployers "control multiple wallets to deploy tokens, distribute funds, and execute trades"; trading bots place "multiple buy/sell orders to artificially increase trading volume" — [Killy7](https://killy7.substack.com/p/serial-token-deployers-on-solana)
- Manipulation signature after graduation: inflated launchpad volume followed by no real buyers on PumpSwap breaks the expected launchpad-to-PumpSwap volume relationship — [TechFlow](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/article/30905) (secondary)

### Inferences
- Reported "volume" on a fresh pump.fun token is nearly uninformative on its own. An agent should compute volume quality metrics instead: unique funded buyers (after funding-cluster dedupe), ratio of round-trip (buy-then-sell by the same wallet within N seconds) volume to total volume, and net SOL inflow to the curve vs. gross volume. High gross volume with low net inflow is the bump-bot fingerprint.
- Wallets with near-continuous 18h+/day activity, thousands of tokens traded, and sub-second round trips are bots; their presence in the top-trader list is normal and not by itself a scam signal, but it means "number of trades" and "number of traders" must not be used as demand proxies.
- The median first-manipulation time of 62 days for Solana tokens (arXiv 2507.01963) refers to tokens that survive; for pump.fun tokens that die within hours, manipulation (bundling, bumping) is concentrated in the first minutes. Both time horizons need separate detectors.

### Gaps
- The widely repeated figure "bots account for 60-80% of pump.fun trading volume" appeared in search summaries but the underlying page (iTiger) returned 404 and no primary analyst/dashboard was identified; treat as unverified.
- The "up to 93% of Solana trading activity is non-organic" claim attributed to Flip Research could not be traced to a primary document; treat as unverified.
- No source gave a per-token false-positive-tested wash-trade detector with published thresholds for Solana memecoins.

## Q5. Sandwich/MEV attacks on retail memecoin trades: frequency, cost, and how apps protect users

### Takeaway
Sandwich attacks are a large, concentrated and still-active drain: Ghostlogs/sandwiched.me estimate $370-500M extracted from Solana DEX users over Jan 2024-May 2025, and Helius/Jito attribute nearly half of all sandwiches to a single operator (DeezNode) that ran 1.55M sandwiches for 65,880 SOL (~$13.4M, ~$8.67 each) in one 30-day window. Solana has no public mempool, so sandwiching depends on validators running private mempools or RPC leakage; defenses are tight slippage, Jito tips/bundles with DontFront, MEV-protect RPC routing that avoids sandwiching validators (Helius), and ecosystem-level validator blacklists (Solana Foundation delegation program, Jito DAO, Marinade).

### Cited Findings
- Ghostlogs (Chris Chang, George Datskos) analyzed 8.5B trades and ~$1T DEX volume: sandwich bots extracted $370-500M over 16 months (Jan 2024-May 2025). "Wide or blind sandwiching" rose from ~1% to ~30% of all sandwich attacks in recent months. Validator "sandwich rate" (blocks with >=1 sandwich / total blocks, 30 days) averages ~5% with outliers at 30-60%. Private mempools replaced Jito's public mempool after its March 2024 shutdown. Sandwich bots tip validators only 5-15% vs. 50-60% for arbitrage bots. Recommended protections: appropriate slippage, avoid high-volatility periods and launches, choose venues by reputation, support validator blacklists — [Solana Compass, Accelerate 2025: State of Solana MEV](https://solanacompass.com/learn/accelerate-25/scale-or-die-at-accelerate-2025-the-state-of-solana-mev)
- Helius report: per Jito's internal analysis, "nearly half of all sandwich attacks on Solana can be attributed to this single program" (DeezNode bot vpeNAL..oax38b). Over Dec 7-Jan 5 (30 days) it executed 1.55M sandwich transactions for 65,880 SOL ($13.43M), ~$8.67 per attack; annualized ~801,540 SOL. DeezNode's validator held 811,604 SOL (~$168.5M) of stake, ~19.89% from Marinade. Recommendations: dynamic slippage and MEV-protection modes in trading apps, sandwich-resistant AMM designs, RFQ for established pairs, Multiple Concurrent Leaders long-term — [Helius, Solana MEV Report](https://helius.dev/blog/solana-mev-report)
- Official Solana guide: although transactions go straight to leaders and expire after ~150 blocks, searchers "with optimized infrastructure can observe transaction flow and exploit ordering"; sandwiches are typically Jito bundles `[frontrun_tx, your_tx, backrun_tx]`. "The single most effective defense against sandwich attacks is limiting acceptable slippage on swaps." Jito DontFront: include a read-only account whose key starts with `jitodontfront` (e.g., `jitodontfront111111111111111111111111111111`) in any instruction and submit via Jito's block engine; the engine then forces any bundle containing the tx to place it at index 0. Jito's caveat: "may help reduce sandwich attacks but is not guaranteed to do so"; does not protect transactions sent directly to validators — [Solana docs, MEV Protection with Jito DontFront](https://solana.com/developers/guides/advanced/mev-protection)
- Helius MEV Protect: flags validators where the validator "includes the sandwich transactions directly" or where "sandwiches repeatedly land in the blocks immediately before or after the validator's," only when statistically significant; excludes roughly "3-4 million SOL" of stake (a small fraction of the network); enabled with `mev-protect=true` on sendTransaction/sendBundle/Sender endpoints; landing-rate and latency impact described as "negligible" for most flows — [Helius docs, MEV Protect](https://www.helius.dev/docs/sending-transactions/mev-protect.md)
- Jito Labs disabled its mempool in March 2024 amid memecoin-era sandwiching; in June 2024 the Solana Foundation removed 30+ validator operators from its delegation program for participating in private mempools that enable sandwiching. Tim Garcia (Solana Foundation): operators "participating in a private mempool to sandwich attack transactions ... will be rejected from the program and any stake from the Foundation will be immediately and permanently removed" — [The Block, Jun 10 2024](https://www.theblock.co/post/299244/solana-foundation-removes-certain-operators-from-delegation-program-over-malicious-sandwich-attacks)
- Underground mempools emerged after Jito's shutdown; the Jito DAO proposed a blacklist of malicious validators excluded from its stake pool; Stakewiz added disclaimers to 51 validators found participating in private mempools — [Blockworks Lightspeed](https://www.blockworks.com/news/lightspeed-newsletter-solana-sandwich-attacks) (page returned 403; claims from search snippet)
- Jito's user-side mitigation is a small tip ("at least around $0.04 per trade during normal network conditions") to route the trade privately through a Jito validator — [DL News](https://www.dlnews.com/articles/defi/solana-users-use-jito-to-stop-sandwich-attacks-and-mev/) (2024 article)
- Community guide tip ranges: 0.0001-0.001 SOL for trades under 1 SOL; 0.001-0.005 SOL for 1-10 SOL; 0.005-0.01 SOL for 10-50 SOL; 0.01-0.05 SOL above 50 SOL; private RPC routing hides the tx from searchers until inclusion — [MadeOnSol, Jito MEV guide](https://madeonsol.com/blog/what-is-jito-mev-solana) (secondary)

### Inferences
- The ~$8.67 average per sandwich (DeezNode) shows sandwiches target mostly small retail trades at scale; for an agent trading, say, 0.5-5 SOL per position with 10-30% slippage on illiquid pump.fun curves, a sandwich can take most of the allowed slippage. High slippage settings are the actual vulnerability; the attacker only profits up to the victim's slippage tolerance.
- Practical defense stack for an agent: (1) set slippage to the minimum that lands (dynamic, based on observed curve depth) rather than a fixed 20-50%; (2) route through an MEV-protect endpoint (Helius `mev-protect=true` or equivalent) or a Jito bundle with a DontFront account and a modest tip; (3) avoid trading in the first blocks of a launch where blind sandwiching and sniping are concentrated; (4) detect sandwiches post hoc by checking whether the same signer bought immediately before and sold immediately after the agent's fill in the same block, and route away from the leader that produced it.
- Private-mempool sandwiching is validator-dependent, so the ~5% average block sandwich rate means most blocks are safe; an agent that tracks leader schedule and known-sandwiching validators (via Helius' or Stakewiz's lists) can simply delay submission by a slot or two when a flagged validator is leader.

### Gaps
- No source gave the share of retail memecoin trades that get sandwiched (only aggregate $ extracted and per-attack average); Ghostlogs explicitly did not publish a per-trade victim-loss distribution.
- No 2026-dated aggregate sandwich statistics were found; latest hard numbers are through May 2025.
- The exact Jito DAO blacklist vote outcome and current list were not retrieved.
- The Chorus One MEV analysis surfaced in search is from April 2022 (found zero confirmed sandwiches in an 8-hour window) and is not representative of 2024-2026.

## Q6. Bait wallets, copy-trade traps and fake social hype

### Takeaway
Copy-trading is a distinct attack surface: adversaries farm "smart money" reputations, hide their positions across bundled wallets, and dump on followers; Luo et al. show every statistics-only wallet-copying baseline produced negative returns after friction, even when it correctly identified profitable wallets. Fake hype (comment bots, paid KOLs, celebrity launches) is pervasive and was central to the MELANIA/LIBRA insider cases.

### Cited Findings
- Luo et al. (Pump.fun, 6,000 projects): copy traders chase "smart money" wallets, but "the extreme illiquidity and high volatility of the meme coin market, combined with the naivete of copy traders, create an ideal environment for exploitation, as sophisticated adversaries deploy automated trading bots to systematically manipulate prices and induce predictable copy-trading behavior." Tactics: position concealment via bundle bots, early-entry predation by snipers, artificial demand signals by bump and comment bots, then coordinated exit once copying occurs. Identified smart-money wallets averaged +14% per trade, yet "all statistic-driven baselines yielded negative returns despite accurate wallet identification"; only their multi-agent (wallet + coin + timing) LLM system achieved ~+3% per trade after friction — [arXiv 2601.08641](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v2)
- KOL "follower farming": KOLs attract inexperienced copy traders, then "promote or manipulate memecoin trades to inflate their own PnLs, leaving copytraders with significant losses when the market shifts or pump-and-dumps occur" — [arXiv 2601.08641 via search summary](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v2)
- Copiers "always execute after the target wallet ... in fast-moving meme tokens, even a one-block delay can mean entering 10-50% higher"; manipulative bots "can conceal their positions, snipe trades, engage in wash trading, and falsify social media signals" — [MadeOnSol, How to Copy Trade on Solana](https://madeonsol.com/blog/how-to-copy-trade-on-solana) (secondary)
- Celebrity/political launches: Bubblemaps and Coffeezilla linked the same wallet entity to sniping MELANIA (~$2.4M) and funding/sniping LIBRA (~$6M, promoted by Argentina's president), using multiple side wallets funded cross-chain to obscure the connection — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/341238/bubblemaps-links-single-entity-to-libra-and-melania-memecoins)
- Comment bots: coordinated, contextless slogan-like hype comments on launch pages; detectable with LLM classification; attention-manipulation bots correlate with superior reported token performance metrics (i.e., the hype works) — [arXiv 2601.08641](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v2)
- Deployers "promote it aggressively via social media, paid influencers, or automated bots, and sell their holdings at peak prices"; most losses come from "coordinated dev wallet sales on the PumpSwap pool" rather than technical hacks — [Killy7](https://killy7.substack.com/p/serial-token-deployers-on-solana)

### Inferences
- "Bait wallet" in practice means a wallet whose public PnL is manufactured (via self-dealing between controlled wallets on illiquid tokens) to attract copiers; its on-chain tell is that its wins come from tokens where the same funding cluster also holds the counterparty supply, and that its exits coincide with bundled dumps. An agent should score candidate wallets on realized PnL against independent counterparties only, and discount wins on tokens where the wallet's cluster held >10% of supply.
- Copy-trading latency alone (one block ~0.4 s) turns a profitable leader into an unprofitable follower on illiquid curves; Luo et al.'s result implies an agent must independently re-validate the token (bundle, sniper, bump, holder checks) and the timing, not just the leader wallet.
- Social signals (comments, X mentions, "CTO" claims) should be treated as adversarial inputs; the only robust use is negative (bot-like hype as a red flag), never positive.

### Gaps
- No quantitative estimate of what share of popular "KOL wallets" on tracking leaderboards are farmed/bait wallets.
- No study found measuring losses specifically attributable to paid-promotion (KOL) campaigns on Solana in 2025-2026; ZachXBT's individual KOL investigations were not surfaced by searches in this session.

## Q7. Which tools/APIs provide these checks, and how reliable are their scores?

### Takeaway
A mature tool stack exists — RugCheck (authorities, LP, insiders, bundlers, score), GoPlus (authority/extension/holder/DEX fields via keyless API), SolSniffer (0-100 Snifscore over 20+ indicators), Bubblemaps (bundle and cluster maps), Mobula (sniper/bundler/insider labels and percentages), Helius (MEV-protect routing), Birdeye/DEXTools (security tabs) — but every reviewer, including the vendors, says composite scores produce false positives/negatives and there is no agreed definition of "passing"; scores should be used as feature inputs, not verdicts.

### Cited Findings
- RugCheck "covers freeze authority, mint authority, bundler activity, insider concentration, developer holdings, and liquidity depth"; its API returns risk score, liquidity, holder distribution, metadata mutability and insider-trading patterns, with separate queries for insiders and lockers; a critical reject flag is "LP unlocked and significant % held by creator" — [Solana Tracker token-safety guide](https://docs.solanatracker.io/guides/token-safety.md); [MadeOnSol RugCheck guide](https://madeonsol.com/blog/how-to-use-rugcheck)
- RugCheck is "the most recognized scanner in the Solana community," fast and account-free, but "has limited historical analysis and doesn't track how token conditions change over time" — [MadeOnSol scanner comparison](https://madeonsol.com/blog/best-solana-token-scanners-rugcheck-solsniffer-defi)
- SolSniffer's Snifscore is a 0-100 proprietary score over "over 20 different security indicators, with plans to expand to 40+"; "the Snifscore is not infallible — false positives occur" — [CryptoScores on Snifscore](https://www.cryptoscores.com/docs/security/solsniffer-token-score); [Bitbond on SolSniffer](https://www.bitbond.com/resources/solsniffer-solanas-security-and-analytics-platform/)
- "Both RugCheck and Token Sniffer produce a single score that can mislead, so treat any score as a starting point, not a verdict"; "Nobody actually agrees on what 'passing' a rug check means in 2026" — [DEXTools tutorial, RugCheck vs Token Sniffer](https://www.dextools.io/tutorials/rugcheck-vs-token-sniffer-comparison) (page 404 on fetch; from search snippet)
- GoPlus Solana Token Security API (Beta) for SPL/SPL-2022: `freezable.status`, `mintable.status`, `balance_mutable_authority`, `closable`, `transfer_fee`, `transfer_hook`, holder data and DEX data; keyless, CORS-open endpoint `https://api.gopluslabs.io/api/v1/solana/token_security?contract_addresses=<MINT>` — [GoPlus docs](https://docs.gopluslabs.io/); [QuickNode](https://www.quicknode.com/fr/builders-guide/tools/goplus-security-by-goplus-labs)
- Mobula: per-holder labels (sniper, bundler, insider), `firstTradeAt`, buy/sell counts and volumes, average entry; token-level `snipersHoldingsPercentage`, `bundlersHoldingsPercentage` — [Mobula docs](https://docs.mobula.io/almanac/detecting-snipers-bundlers)
- Bubblemaps: bundle checker from launch-block data, cluster maps by funding/transfer relationships, risk thresholds as in Q3; rebuilt platform positioned to "screen out rug pulls before traders ever see the token" — [Bubblemaps blog](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/how-to-analyze-meme-coin-holders-with-bubblemaps/); [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/bubblemaps-rebuilds-its-platform-to-screen-out-rug-pulls-before-traders-ever-see-the-token)
- Jupiter and RugCheck surface a permanent-delegate indicator since the Sept 2024 burn scam — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/new-solana-scam-burn-tokens-from-inside-sol-wallets)
- Helius: MEV Protect routing (validator exclusion) and Transaction Rebates; Pine Analytics' heuristics (first 20-50 block concentration, repeat-offender labels, behavioral trust scoring) — [Helius docs](https://www.helius.dev/docs/sending-transactions/mev-protect.md); [Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines)
- Open-source/self-hosted options: decode mint authority, freeze authority and Token-2022 extension bytes directly from the mint account — [dev.to](https://dev.to/verixia_233e4721792ce3390/programmatic-spl-token-risk-analysis-inspecting-mint-freeze-and-token-2022-extension-bytes-l0d); scanners that check top-20 token accounts for frozen state — [Apify](https://apify.com/sanjeep/solana-token-safety-checker?fpr=data)

### Inferences
- Deterministic checks (authorities, Token-2022 extensions, LP lock/burn status, frozen top accounts) are reliable across all tools because they read on-chain state directly; disagreement and false positives concentrate in the probabilistic parts (insider labels, bundle percentages, composite scores). An agent should pull raw fields from at least two providers (e.g., GoPlus + RugCheck, or its own RPC decode) and apply its own thresholds rather than trusting any single 0-100 score.
- No vendor publishes precision/recall against a labelled rug dataset; the only audited detector found is the academic preprint (0.26% FP), which is retrospective, not real-time. Treat vendor scores as unvalidated.
- Score gaming is likely: since pump.fun launches already revoke mint/freeze by default and most dumps are pure holder-cluster plays, a "clean" authority score is the norm for scam tokens too; the discriminating signals are cluster/bundle/funding analysis and deployer history, which the simpler scanners either omit or compute shallowly.

### Gaps
- Birdeye security-endpoint field list could not be retrieved (docs URL redirected then 404).
- No independent benchmark comparing RugCheck, SolSniffer, GoPlus and Bubblemaps labels on the same token set was found.
- DEXTools' own Solana audit methodology page was not retrieved.

## Q8. Synthesized pre-buy on-chain checklist for an AI trading agent (defensive)

### Takeaway
Combining the above, a buyer-side agent should run a fixed sequence of cheap deterministic checks, then cluster/bundle analysis, then execution hygiene, and should default to "no" given 75-98% base rates of failure/fraud.

### Cited Findings
- Thresholds and signals used below are drawn from: Bubblemaps bundle (<5 / 5-15 / >15 / >30%) and cluster (<15 / 15-30 / 30-50 / >50%) tiers — [Bubblemaps](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/how-to-analyze-meme-coin-holders-with-bubblemaps/); single bundle >10% red flag — [Bubblemaps](https://blog.bubblemaps.io/whats-the-difference-between-bundle-cluster-2/); Mobula sniper/bundler signals — [Mobula](https://docs.mobula.io/almanac/detecting-snipers-bundlers); holder rules (top-10 <40% ex-LP, no wallet >10%, creator <5%) — [Solana Tracker](https://docs.solanatracker.io/guides/token-safety.md); sniper exit timing (55% within 60 s) — [Pine Analytics](https://pineanalytics.substack.com/p/exit-liquidity-machines); slippage as primary sandwich defense and DontFront — [Solana docs](https://solana.com/developers/guides/advanced/mev-protection); MEV-protect routing — [Helius](https://www.helius.dev/docs/sending-transactions/mev-protect.md); bump-bot ratio heuristic — [arXiv 2601.08641](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.08641v2); frozen top-account check — [Apify](https://apify.com/sanjeep/solana-token-safety-checker?fpr=data)

### Inferences
Tier 0 — hard rejects (one RPC call on the mint + holder scan):
1. Token program is Token-2022 with any of: permanent delegate, transfer fee, transfer hook, non-transferable, or confidential transfer enabled -> reject.
2. Mint authority or freeze authority not null -> reject (for memecoins there is no legitimate reason).
3. Any of the top 20 token accounts in `frozen` state -> reject (active honeypot).
4. Post-graduation (PumpSwap/Raydium) pool where LP tokens are neither burned nor locked and are held by the creator cluster -> reject.
5. Mutable metadata with update authority held by the creator is a soft flag (token can be re-skinned), not a reject.

Tier 1 — launch-structure analysis (transaction history of first ~5 blocks + funding graph):
6. Compute bundled supply: buys in creation block and next 5 blocks, grouped by shared transaction/Jito bundle and by shared SOL funder within 1-3 hops. Reject if >15% of supply (strong) or any single bundle >10%; flag at 5-15%.
7. Deployer behavior: number of prior tokens deployed (serial deployers), prior tokens' outcomes, whether deployer still holds supply, whether deployer-linked wallets received tokens or SOL before launch. Maintain a persistent blacklist of deployers and wallets seen in prior bundles/dumps.
8. Cluster concentration: connected-wallet supply >30% -> reject; 15-30% -> size position down or wait.

Tier 2 — volume quality (recent trades):
9. Round-trip ratio: volume from wallets that buy and sell within seconds / total volume; high ratio with low net SOL inflow = bump bots; treat volume as zero signal.
10. Unique funded buyers after cluster dedupe, not raw trader count.
11. After graduation: PumpSwap net inflow should be consistent with bonding-curve inflow; a cliff indicates fake curve volume.

Tier 3 — execution hygiene:
12. Dynamic minimum slippage based on current curve/pool depth; never a fixed high slippage.
13. Route via MEV-protect RPC or Jito bundle with a DontFront account and modest tip; avoid submitting when a known sandwiching validator is leader.
14. Do not buy in the first ~60 seconds of a launch (sniper exit window); if entering later, confirm bundled wallets have already sold.
15. Post-trade: check for same-block front/back-run by the same signer; if detected, mark the leader and adjust routing.

Tier 4 — social/leader signals: use only negatively (bot-like comment hype, farmed KOL wallet with wins only against its own cluster = red flag); never as a positive signal.

### Gaps
- These thresholds are vendor and community heuristics; no source provided out-of-sample validation of a combined checklist's hit rate on 2025-2026 pump.fun launches. The one academic end-to-end system (Luo et al.) reports ~+3% per trade after friction for copy-trading, which is the only quantified "it can work" datapoint found.
